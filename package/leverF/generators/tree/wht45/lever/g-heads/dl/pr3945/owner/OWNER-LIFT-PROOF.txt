1. A sufficient owner-lifting theorem

Fix a finite ordered set of calls. Each helper role is assigned to an owner,
an idle child and a port, whose orthogonal coordinate block has capacity c.
Assume the old assignment satisfies its strict lower / closed upper time
window, uses distinct physical families within a call, and allocates disjoint
coordinate intervals by chronological prefix offsets.

Increase the width w_i of each role to w_i + d_i, where d_i is nonnegative.
Suppose that, in every owner/child/port block, the sum of its d_i is at most
its previously unused capacity. Retain the owner, child, port and call order;
replace every offset by the sum of the new widths of its preceding roles.
Then all new intervals fit, remain disjoint, and preserve every time-window
and same-call physical-family condition.

Proof. The last new endpoint in a block is its old occupied length plus the
sum of the increases. The hypothesis bounds this by c. Every earlier prefix
is no larger, because all widths are nonnegative. Consecutive prefix intervals
are disjoint by construction. The call time and physical family depend only
on the retained identities, not on the offset, so their predicates persist.
This proves an owner-placement result. Native phases and the caller reduction
require their separate source-bound checks.

2. The literal instance

Both new providers have the same local paid profile

  2482 H_1 + 712 H_2 + 218 H_3 + 109 H_4 + 20 H_5 + 8 H_8.

The old profile was

  2440 H_1 + 725 H_2 + 224 H_3 + 102 H_4 + 20 H_5 + 8 H_8.

Exactly 26 helper widths increase from 4 to 5 in each call. The new inventory
is (width : count) 5 : 28, 6 : 18, 7 : 53, 8 : 96, 9 : 213.
There are 9 calls per replica and 360 replicas. The owner checker examines
all 1,321,920 roles and all 725,760 owner/child/port blocks. Every one of the
84,240 changed blocks had precisely one unused coordinate and gains precisely
one occupied coordinate. Thus its idle rank changes from 1 to 0. All other
idle ranks, 3, 5 and 48, are unchanged. ORDER and every role's first five
identity fields are unchanged. CHECK.json binds the exact inputs and records
the full chronology, distinctness, capacity and mutation-control checks.

The complete new paid histogram is

  H_1 : 8041680       H_2 : 2306880       H_3 : 729648
  H_4 : 353160        H_5 : 69120         H_8 : 25920
  H_48 : 137952.

Its difference from the old complete histogram, per replica, is exactly

  144 H_1 − 117 H_2 − 54 H_3 + 63 H_4.

The rank mass of this difference is 144 − 234 − 162 + 252 = 0.

3. A uniform strict gain, without numerical root isolation

For 0 < κ ≤ 1/500 put s = 1 − κ and

  D(s) = 16 − 13 · 2^s − 6 · 3^s + 7 · 4^s.

Then the new minus old moment per replica is 9 D(1 − κ). We prove

  9 D(1 − κ) ≤ −(122944077 / 15625000) κ < 0.

First D(1) = 0. For 499/500 ≤ s ≤ 1,

  D′(s) = −13 (log 2) 2^s − 6 (log 3) 3^s + 7 (log 4) 4^s.

Use 693/1000 < log 2 < 694/1000 and
1098/1000 < log 3 < 1099/1000. These are elementary rigorous bounds: expand
log((1+x)/(1−x)) = 2 Σ_{j≥0} x^(2j+1)/(2j+1), taking three terms for x = 1/3
and six for x = 1/2. For m retained terms, the positive remainder is at most
2 x^(2m+1) / ((2m+1)(1−x²)). All resulting comparisons are rational.

Because e^(−x) ≥ 1 − x for x ≥ 0,

  4^s ≥ 4(1 − (1/500)(1388/1000)) = 498612/125000.

Consequently

  D′(s)
  ≥ 7 (693/500)(498612/125000)
       − 26 (347/500) − 18 (1099/1000)
  = 13660453/15625000 > 0.

Integrating this inequality from 1 − κ to 1 proves the stated strict gain.
The exact rational arithmetic and the logarithm-series bounds are replayed
by check_gain.py. In particular, every previously admissible positive κ at
most 0.002 obtains a strictly smaller moment under this literal substitution.
Continuity gives a strictly larger admissible endpoint whenever all retained
construction/caller hypotheses are supplied. A numerical value requires the
separate outward-rounded root certificate; no new endpoint is asserted here.
