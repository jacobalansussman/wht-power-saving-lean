## 0. Setting

Block 3, the chi game (running XOR `row ^= row << 1` free). At the alpha reading (xc3-cert3 §2, [read]) Pauli labels are
F_4^6 = {(x | z)}, with the Hermitian form h(u, v) = Σ_i ū_{x,i} v_{z,i} + ū_{z,i} v_{x,i}; frames are the 891 generators
(totally isotropic 3-spaces) of H(5,4); d(U, U') = 3 − dim(U ∩ U'). L0 = {x = 0} is the frame of every free letter,
M0 = {z = 0} = K L0. An *alpha-route certificate* is τ ∘ π_α: a representation τ of the symbol group Γ_α (generated by
the alpha-symbols of the letters) on a complex space X, and a subspace N ⊂ X kept by the symbols of the free letters;
cap = f_N(direction), dem = f_N(K), f_N(g) = dim(N + gN) − dim N. Its validity as a lower-bound certificate needs S1/S2
(xc3-s1s2 Theorem S, [read]: proved there for Clifford letters regular at alpha) and Theorem R-infinity; the CEILINGS below
need none of that: they bound the number dem/cap for every such (τ, N).

## 1. Lemma I1-α (the interval) [proved; checked by BFS, `interval891.py` → `out_interval891.txt`]

The frames U with d(L0, U) + d(U, M0) = 3 are exactly N_s = {(x|0) : x ∈ s} ⊕ {(0|z) : z ∈ s^⊥}, one for each F_4-subspace
s of F_4^3, where s^⊥ = {z : Σ x̄_i z_i = 0 for all x ∈ s}. Moreover d(N_s, N_t) = dim(s+t) − dim(s ∩ t). There are
1 + 21 + 21 + 1 = 44 such frames and 21 + 105 + 21 = 147 covering pairs (0 ⋖ point, point ⋖ line, line ⋖ F_4^3).

*Proof.* d(L0,U) + d(U,M0) = 6 − dim(U∩L0) − dim(U∩M0). Since L0 ∩ M0 = 0, the two intersections are independent
subspaces of U, so the sum is 3 iff U = (U∩M0) ⊕ (U∩L0). Write U∩M0 = s × 0 and U∩L0 = 0 × b. Isotropy of U gives
h((x|0),(0|z)) = Σ x̄_i z_i = 0 for x ∈ s, z ∈ b, i.e. b ⊂ s^⊥; dim b = 3 − dim s = dim s^⊥ (the form Σ x̄_i z_i is
non-degenerate), so b = s^⊥. Conversely N_s is totally isotropic (the cross terms vanish by the choice of s^⊥, and
h((x|0),(x'|0)) = h((0|z),(0|z')) = 0) of dimension 3. Finally N_s ∩ N_t = (s∩t) × (s^⊥ ∩ t^⊥) = (s∩t) × (s+t)^⊥, so
dim(N_s ∩ N_t) = dim(s∩t) + 3 − dim(s+t). ∎
The BFS check (`out_interval891.txt`) finds the same 44 frames by distances in the 891-frame model, verifies the distance
formula on all 44² pairs and counts the 147 edges.

**Symmetry** [computed, `out_interval891.txt`]. Inside Γ_α: K (x|z) ↦ (z|x) maps N_s to N_{s^⊥} (swaps L0 and M0);
E(α) = diag(A, A^{-*}) with A = e_01(α), and the CNOT-type elements diag(e_ij(1), e_ji(1)), map N_s to N_{As}; with the
shift symbol they give PGL(3,4) on s, so Γ_α induces PGL(3,4) ⋊ ⟨K⟩ (order 120960) on the interval. The Frobenius
(coordinatewise squaring) also preserves d but is not induced by Γ_α (it fixes the 7 points of a Baer subplane and moves
the other 14, so it is not in PGL(3,4)). The full group of d-preserving permutations is PΓL(3,4) ⋊ 2, order 241920;
every LP below is symmetrised over it, which is free by Lemma Sym (§3).

## 2. Theorem I-α (certificates are uniform families) [proved, given the facts of §0]

Every alpha-route certificate (τ, N) gives a family U ↦ Y_U on the 891 frames, and by restriction on the interval, with
δ(U, V) := dim(Y_U + Y_V) − dim Y_V = δ_{d(U,V)} depending only on the distance, δ_1 = cap and δ_3 = dem.

*Proof.* Pick γ_U ∈ Γ_α with γ_U L0 = U and put Y_U = τ(γ_U)N; this does not depend on the choice, because two choices
differ by an element of the stabiliser P of L0, which is generated by free symbols (and scalars) and keeps N. Then
δ(U, V) = f_N(γ_V^{-1} γ_U). If d(γL0, L0) = d(γ'L0, L0), transitivity of Γ_α on ordered pairs at that distance gives
g ∈ Γ_α with g L0 = L0 and g γ L0 = γ' L0, so γ ∈ P γ' P; f_N is P-bi-invariant (Lemma 1.1 of pf-duality, `proofs/duality/
PROOF.md`), so f_N(γ) depends only on d(γL0, L0). A direction moves L0 to distance 1 and K to distance 3. ∎

So every alpha-route certificate proves at most Q_α^unif := sup over uniform families on the 44-frame interval (over
fields of characteristic 0) of δ_3/δ_1.

**Corollary C-α** [proved]. Over F_4 the family Y_s = N_s is uniform with δ = d (Lemma I1-α), ratio exactly 3. So any upper
bound below 3 obtained from rank inequalities valid over every field is impossible: the proof must use rows false in
characteristic 2 (DFZ, dual DFZ). (Same proof as pf-duality Corollary C.)

## 3. Theorem A (the ceiling at alpha): every alpha-route certificate proves at most 169/57 = 2.9649 moves per array

Three proofs, each enough.

**(i) Game monotonicity** [proved, given pf-duality Theorem D3 and the game inclusion of MAP §1.4]. The chi game contains
the engine game: the same paid letters (column-uniform directions) and the same kernel K, and more free letters. A
certificate (ρ, N) of the chi game (any route, not only alpha) restricted to the subgroup generated by the engine letters
is an engine-game certificate with the same f_N(H_1) and f_N(K): N is kept by the engine's free letters because they are
free in the chi game too. Hence BEST^chi ≤ BEST^engine_3 ≤ 169/57 (Theorem D3). *Ceilings flow up the game lattice,
as lower bounds flow down.* This covers every chi-game certificate, not only those on the alpha reading.

**(ii) Baer restriction** [proved]. The standard Baer sub-interval {N_s : s spanned by vectors with entries in F_2}
(16 frames: L0, the 7 points and 7 lines of the Baer subplane PG(2,2) ⊂ PG(2,4), and M0) is isometric to the block-3
interval of the engine game: for F_2-rational s, t the F_4-dimensions of s + t and s ∩ t equal the F_2-dimensions of
their F_2-parts. A uniform family on the alpha interval restricts to a uniform family on it, with the same δ_1, δ_2, δ_3.
Theorem D3's 18-row certificate (`proofs/duality/cert_m/certificate_m3_K6_u_1.json`) uses only frames of that interval,
rows valid for every family in characteristic ≠ 2, and uniformity equations among pairs of equal distance, so it applies
verbatim: δ_3 ≤ (169/57) δ_1. The same argument works at every reading whose interval is the subspace lattice of F_q^3
with q a power of 2 (F_2 ⊂ F_q), e.g. q = 16 for the readings at elements of order 5.

**(iii) A native certificate at alpha** [proved: exact certificate checked by an independent program]. The LP of §4 at
q = 4 returns a 13-row dual certificate (`certificate_q4_m3_K5_u_1D.json`): 5 submodular, 4 monotone, 3 Ingleton rows and
one DFZ row (Dougherty, Freiling and Zeger, "Achievable Rate Regions for Network Coding", arXiv:1311.4601, 2013, Theorem 8.6, inequality (65), stated for odd characteristic; valid over C by pf-duality Lemma
L(iii) [read]) with multipliers 11/19, 28/57, 14/57 (Ingleton) and 2/57 (DFZ), plus 5 uniformity equations, giving
mean δ_3 ≤ (169/57) mean δ_1. `verify_q.py` rebuilds PG(2,4) from scratch (points as normalised vectors, frames as point
sets, the group of order 241920 from all invertible 3×3 matrices over F_4 with and without Frobenius and the Hermitian
polarity), rebuilds every row from the explicit frames written in the certificate, and checks that the combination has
every coefficient ≥ 0: `out_verify_q4m3K5.txt` says PROVED. The symmetrisation over PΓL(3,4) ⋊ 2 is free by pf-duality's
Lemma Sym (its proof only uses that each group element preserves d, which `verify_q.py` checks on a sample and which holds
for collineations, the Frobenius and correlations by Lemma I1-α).

**Lemma G-α** [computed, `decode_q.py` → `out_decode_q4.txt`]. As in pf-duality Lemma G, 3 − 169/57 = 2/57 equals the

## 5. Lifts

A *lift* of the interval assigns to each s a 3-dimensional subspace Y_s ⊂ Q(ω)^6 spanned by the rows of a matrix over Z[ω]
whose reduction mod 2 (Z[ω]/2Z[ω] = F_4, ω ↦ α; 2 is inert) is a basis of N_s. Rank over Q(ω) of a Z[ω]-matrix is at least
the F_4-rank of its reduction, so δ ≥ d on every pair, δ = 3 on opposite pairs, and on a covering edge δ = 1 + e(s,t) with
an integer excess e ≥ 0. Ratio of an interval lift: 3/(1 + E/147), E = Σ_edges e (pf-duality Lemma I2, same proof).

**Lemma L2 (local to global)** [proved, given the facts of §0]. Every lift of the 891-frame graph (3-dim Y_U reducing to
U) has ratio ā_3/ā_1 = 3/(1 + E/18711) ≤ 3/(1 + b_α/147), where b_α is the least total excess of an interval lift.
*Proof.* Every opposite pair (U, V) spans an interval [U, V] (a Γ_α-image of [L0, M0], by transitivity on pairs at
distance 3, so Lemma I1-α applies) with 147 covering edges, and the global lift restricted to it is an interval lift, so
its excess there is ≥ b_α. Γ_α maps intervals to intervals and is transitive on edges, so every edge lies in the same
number c of intervals; counting incidences, c · 18711 = 147 · #intervals and c · E = Σ_intervals E[U,V] ≥ #intervals · b_α.
Divide. ∎

**Lemma B (Fano obstruction for lifts, refined)** [proved]. In every lift, for every Baer subplane B (7 points, 7 lines of
PG(2,4) each containing 3 of them): some edge among the 7 edges (L0, p), p ∈ B, and the 21 flags (p, ℓ), p ∈ ℓ, ℓ a line
of B, has positive excess; and likewise some edge among those 21 flags and the 7 edges (ℓ, M0).
*Proof.* Suppose the 28 low edges are clean. Write Y_0 = Y_{L0}, π: Q(ω)^6 → Q(ω)^6/Y_0 ≅ Q(ω)^3.
(1) dim(Y_0 ∩ Y_p) = 2 (clean edge), so a_p := π(Y_p) is a line (projective point).
(2) For a line ℓ of B and p ∈ ℓ: dim(Y_p ∩ Y_ℓ) = 2. Also dim(Y_0 + Y_ℓ) ≥ F_4-rank of N_0 + N_ℓ = 5, so
dim(Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ) ≤ 1. The 2-spaces U = Y_0 ∩ Y_p and V = Y_p ∩ Y_ℓ of the 3-space Y_p are different (else U ⊂ Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ) and
meet in a line inside Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ; hence dim(Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ) = 1, π(Y_ℓ) is a plane (projective line) λ_ℓ, and Y_p = U + V ⊂
Y_0 + Y_ℓ, so a_p ⊂ λ_ℓ.
(3) Distinct points of B: dim π(Y_p + Y_q) ≥ F_4-rank(N_0 + N_p + N_q) − 3 = 2, so a_p ≠ a_q. Three non-collinear points of
B: dim π(Y_p + Y_q + Y_r) ≥ 6 − 3 = 3.
So the seven a_p are distinct points of P^2(Q(ω)), the three on each line of B are collinear, and non-collinear triples are
not: a representation of the Fano matroid over a field of characteristic 0, which does not exist (three points on the
"diagonal" line would force 2 = 0; e.g. w-bounds Theorem F's coordinates). The second statement follows by applying the
first to the lift s ↦ K Y_{s^⊥} (K maps the interval to itself, swapping L0, M0 and points with lines, and maps Baer
subplanes to Baer subplanes). ∎
(The coarse form, "some edge among all 35 edges of the Baer configuration", is xc3-cert3's Fano count.)

**Corollary B** (b_α ≥ 5 [proved]; b_α ≥ 9 [computed]). Each of the 105 flags lies in 72 Baer subplanes and each of the
21 + 21 outer edges in 120 (computed, `out_transversal.txt`; also by flag-transitivity: 360·21/105, 360·7/21). Summing the
720 inequalities of Lemma B over the indicator x_e of "e has positive excess": 144 Σ_flags x + 120 Σ_outer x ≥ 720, so
Σ_e x_e ≥ 5 and E ≥ 5. The integer program (147 binaries, 720 covering rows; `transversal.py`, HiGHS MILP, status
optimal) has minimum 9, attained by 9 flags; so E ≥ 9 for every lift [computed; an independent check of the MILP was not
done]. Consequence (Lemma L2): every lift on the 891 frames has ratio ≤ 3/(1 + 5/147) = 441/152 = 2.901 [proved] and
≤ 3/(1 + 9/147) = 147/52 = 2.827 [computed].

**Lemma B2 (finer local facts)** [proved]. Write Y_0 = Y_{L0}, Y_F = Y_{M0}. For a point p and a line ℓ:
(a) Y_0 ∩ Y_F = 0, dim(Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ) ≤ 1, dim(Y_p ∩ Y_F) ≤ 1 (reductions: N_0 + N_ℓ and N_p + N_F have F_4-rank 6, 5, 5).
(b) If the bottom edge (L0, p) and the flag (p, ℓ) are clean, then dim(Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ) = 1 and Y_p ⊂ Y_0 + Y_ℓ (proof of Lemma B).
(c) Hence if (L0, p) is clean and dim(Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ) = 0, the flag (p, ℓ) has excess ≥ 1. Mirror: if (ℓ, M0) is clean and
dim(Y_p ∩ Y_F) = 0, the flag has excess ≥ 1.
(d) If (L0, p) and (ℓ, M0) are clean and the flag (p, ℓ) has excess exactly 1, then Y_p ⊂ Y_0 + Y_ℓ or Y_ℓ ⊂ Y_F + Y_p.
*Proof of (d).* Excess 1 means Y_p ∩ Y_ℓ = ⟨w⟩ is a line. By (a) w ∉ Y_0 or w ∉ Y_F. If w ∉ Y_0, then
Y_p = (Y_p ∩ Y_0) ⊕ ⟨w⟩ ⊂ Y_0 + Y_ℓ (dim(Y_p ∩ Y_0) = 2 as (L0,p) is clean). If w ∉ Y_F, then Y_ℓ = (Y_ℓ ∩ Y_F) ⊕ ⟨w⟩
⊂ Y_F + Y_p. ∎
*Low and high pictures.* π: Q(ω)^6 → Q(ω)^6/Y_0 ≅ Q(ω)^3 ⊂ C^3. For p with clean bottom, a_p = π(Y_p) is a point of
P^2(C); write U_ℓ = 1 if dim(Y_0 ∩ Y_ℓ) = 0, else λ_ℓ = π(Y_ℓ) is a line. A *broken low incidence* at the flag (p, ℓ)
means: bottom at p clean, U_ℓ = 0 and a_p ∉ λ_ℓ (Ls = 1). Mirror (quotient by Y_F): b_ℓ, μ_p, V_p, broken high incidence
(Hs = 1). By (b) and (d): **excess(p, ℓ) ≥ Ls + Hs** — an excess-1 flag breaks at most one of the two incidences.
(e) Realised configurations: the a_p are distinct, and a_p, a_q, a_r collinear in P^2(C) implies p, q, r collinear in
PG(2,4) (rank of a reduction). So every line of P^2(C) through two realised points a_p, a_q contains realised points of
the PG(2,4)-line pq only; and if U_ℓ = 0 the points of ℓ without a broken low incidence lie on λ_ℓ.

**Lemma B3 (counting rows)** [proved, given Hirzebruch's inequality]. Let K = number of bad bottom edges, and for a line
ℓ let k_ℓ = bad bottoms on ℓ and j_ℓ = broken low incidences on ℓ.
(i) Fano: every Baer subplane B has a bad bottom at a point of B, or U_ℓ = 1 for a line of B, or a broken low incidence
at one of its 21 flags (Lemma B's proof needs exactly the negation). Mirror for the high side.
(ii) Whole plane. Hirzebruch's inequality (F. Hirzebruch, "Arrangements of lines and algebraic surfaces", 1983; cited
from memory, also used by wave17/xc3-s1s2 §7): n points of P^2(C), at most n − 3 on a line, have
t_2 + (3/4) t_3 ≥ n + Σ_{r≥5} (r − 4) t_r, t_r = number of lines with exactly r of the points. Apply it to the
n = 21 − K realised points (valid for K ≤ 13: then n ≥ 8 and at most 5 on a line). By (e) the counted lines split by
PG(2,4)-lines; put ψ_ℓ = 1 + [t_2 + (3/4) t_3 − Σ_{r≥5} (r−4) t_r restricted to the lines inside ℓ]. The ideal
configuration (a 5-point line) has ψ_ℓ = 0. Maximising over the ways the 5 − k_ℓ realised points can sit (j_ℓ of them
off λ_ℓ, or no λ_ℓ when U_ℓ = 1; extra collinearities only lower the count) gives the table
ψ(k, j): (0,0) 0; (0,1) 5; (0,2) 8.75; (0,≥3) 11; (1,0) 1; (1,1) 4.75; (1,≥2) 7; (2,0) 1.75; (2,≥1) 4; (3,·) 2; (4,·), (5,·) 1;
U_ℓ = 1: C(5−k,2) + 1. All are ≤ min(k + 5j + 11U, 2 + 5j + 11U). Hirzebruch gives Σ_ℓ ψ_ℓ ≥ 42 − K.
(iii) Affine planes. For a line ℓ0, the 16 points off ℓ0 with the 20 other lines (4 points each): with
φ ≤ min(0.75k + 3.75j + 6U, 1 + 3.75j + 6U) per line, Σ φ ≥ 16 − K_aff (valid for K_aff ≤ 8).
(iv) Mirror rows on the high side (top edges, V, broken high incidences; dual affine planes for each point p0).
Every lift therefore gives a feasible point of the integer program `transversal3.py` with objective ≤ its total excess.

**Lemma L3 (conditional gluing bound)** [proved, conditional on the hypothesis]. Suppose every interval lift with o bad
edges in outer position (incident to the interval's two ends) has total excess ≥ 18 + g·o, for some constant g > 0.
Then every lift on the 891 frames has E/18711 ≥ (18 + (18/7) g)/147; for g ≥ 1/6 this exceeds 18.375/147, so no lift
excludes 3 in 8. *Proof.* E/18711 = (mean over intervals of E_I)/147 (double counting, as in Lemma L2). By

so mean o_I ≥ (42/147)·9 = 18/7, and mean E_I ≥ 18 + g · 18/7. ∎ (Without a hypothesis of this kind the local bounds
cannot decide gluing.)

## 6. What is not proved here

- That Q_α^unif < 169/57, or any lower bound on Q_α^unif beyond 231/131 (the best whole-graph family, xc3-cert3).
- That the LP cannot be pushed lower by other rows (the cut search is heuristic; other characteristic-dependent rank
  inequalities exist, e.g. those of DFZ's other theorems, not tried).
- That frames outside the interval add nothing (the whole-graph LP on 891 frames was not run: the symmetry group is too
  large to list as permutations, and the orbit computation would need a stabiliser-chain canonical form).
- The value of b_α: only 12 ≤ b_α ≤ 18. That b_α = 18, and that every E = 18 interval lift has the tangent-flag pattern,
  are [guess]; with both, a gluing count over intervals becomes possible (NOTE.md, Next).
