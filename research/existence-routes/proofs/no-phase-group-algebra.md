## 0. Setting and notation

Block m. Paid letter `dir(z) = ((1+i)[1] + (1-i)[X^z])/2` as a matrix, a group element in the formal sense. Free letters
of the engine game E: shifts `X^a`, address maps `P_h` (h in GL(m,2)), phase letters `i^{[w.x + c]}`. A word is right
for every f exactly when it is a formal identity in C[G] (w-engine GAME.md section 5). The commutative slice A is the
group generated by the `dir(z)` and the shifts; it is abelian. For a group H of address maps, write
`G_H = A x| H` (slice plus shifts plus the address maps in H, no phases) and `B_H = X x| H` for its free part
(X = the shift group, isomorphic to F_2^m). `G_1` is the commutative slice; `G_GL` is the whole engine game without
phase letters.

The U_m target: `W = 2^(m-2)` arrays, `M = 2^m - 1` moves (4 in 15 at block 4, 8 in 31 at block 5).

*Module form of a unit* [proved, standard]. Contents are row vectors in `C[G]^W`; every step is a left
multiplication or a scalar mixing. So a W-in-M word exists iff there are `v_1, ..., v_M` with `v_t` in
`L_{t-1}` and `K e_l` in `L_M` for every l, where `L_0 = C[B] e_1 + ... + C[B] e_W` and
`L_t = L_{t-1} + C[B] dir(z_t) v_t` (left C[B]-spans). Since `dir(hz) = P_h dir(z) P_h^-1`, a move in direction z can
be replaced by a move in direction hz at no cost when P_h is free; with H transitive on nonzero directions every move
may be taken in direction e_1.

## Lemma S (shift sectors split every program into independent blocks) [proved]

For `mu` in F_2^m let `e_mu = 2^-m sum_a (-1)^{mu.a} [X^a]` (a free element). In `C[G_H]`:
(a) the `e_mu` are orthogonal idempotents summing to 1, they commute with every `dir(z)` and with every shift, and
    `P_h e_mu P_h^-1 = e_{h^-T mu}`;
(b) hence the sum of `e_mu` over an H-orbit O of F_2^m is a central idempotent `e_O` of `C[G_H]` lying in `C[B_H]`;
(c) a unit with moves `z_1..z_M` restricts to a unit of every block `C[G_H] e_O`, with the same moves.
In particular `e_0` is central (the orbit {0}), so **every unit of `G_H` gives a unit of the same shape (same W, M and
directions) in the sector-0 block `C[G_H] e_0`**.

*Proof.* (a) The `X^a` form a group isomorphic to F_2^m, so the `e_mu` are its character idempotents. A is abelian, so
they commute with A; `P_h X^a P_h^-1 = X^{ha}` gives the conjugation rule. (b) Conjugation by `P_h` permutes the
`e_mu` within O. (c) Write the word as `Lambda_M D_M ... D_1 Lambda_0` with `D_t = I + e_{r_t} (dir(z_t) - 1) e_{r_t}^T`
and free `Lambda`'s. Multiplying every input by `e_O` commutes with every step (e_O central), so the cut word computes
`K e_O` on the block. (A converse is not needed: for the sector-0 block Theorem A gives one directly.) []

## Lemma R (the corrected move is an involution; all sectors look alike) [proved; raw-array check]

Let `c_z = ((1-i)[1] + (1+i)[X^z])/2` (free) and `d'_z = c_z dir(z)`. Then:
(a) at f digit columns, in the Hadamard (X) basis, `d'_z` is the diagonal sign `(-1)^{binom(s,2)}` with
    `s = #{columns c : z.y_c = 1}`; so `d'_z` is an involution, the `d'_z` commute, `P_h d'_z P_h^-1 = d'_{hz}`, and
    `c_z` is invertible (`c_z^2 = X^z`);
(b) in every shift sector mu, at the character `chi_{mu,Q}` (`chi(dir w) = i^{[mu.w]} (-1)^{Q(w)}`, Q a binary quadratic
    form), `d'_z` acts as `(-1)^{Q(z)}`, independently of mu;
(c) `C[A] = C[X] (x) C[A']` with `A' = <d'_z>` isomorphic to the additive group Sym_m(F_2) of symmetric matrices, via
    `d'_z -> z z^T`; H acts on Sym_m by `S -> h S h^T`. The kernel is `K = (prod_j c_{e_j})^-1 prod_j d'_{e_j}`, a free
    unit times `[I]`.

*Proof.* (a) In the X basis `dir(z)` is `i^s` and `X^z` is `(-1)^s` on the f-tuple y. So `d'_z` acts as
`i^s ((1-i) + (1+i)(-1)^s)/2`, that is `i^s` for even s and `i^s (-i)` for odd s: 1 for s = 0, 1 and -1 for s = 2, 3
(mod 4), which is `(-1)^{binom(s,2)}`. The rest follows (diagonal signs commute and square to 1; `c_z^2 = X^z` by the same evaluation).
(b) At `chi_{mu,Q}` the value of `dir(z)` is `i^{q}` with `q = [mu.z] + 2Q(z)` (mod 4), and `X^z` is `(-1)^{mu.z}`.
So `d'_z` gives `i^q ((1-i) + (1+i)(-1)^{mu.z})/2`; for `mu.z = 0` this is `(-1)^{Q(z)}`, for `mu.z = 1` it is
`i^{1+2Q} (-i) = (-1)^{Q(z)}`. (c) By (b) the `d'_z` act on each sector block `C[A] e_mu` (dimension 2^{C(m+1,2)},
characters Q) exactly as `zz^T` acts on C[Sym_m] (characters `S -> (-1)^{<Q,S>}`, `Q(z) = <Q, zz^T>`); the sector
blocks are indexed by C[X]. []

its own block (Lemma S) and, after the free substitution `dir(z) -> d'_z`, every block is the same system: the order-2

**Corollary R2 (the address-map game in normal form)** [proved]. `G_H` is the game on `(X x Sym_m) x| H` with moves
`[zz^T]` (times a free unit), target `[I]` (times a free unit) and free part `C[X x| H]`; X acts trivially on Sym_m. Its
sector-0 block is the problem `R_0(H)`: the group algebra `C[Sym_m x| H]`, moves `[zz^T]`, free `C[H]`, target `[I]`.

## Theorem A (the engine game without phase letters is a group algebra of symmetric matrices) [proved]

For every group H of address maps: the game `G_H` (commutative slice, shifts, the address maps in H, no phase
letters) has a W-in-M unit with move directions `z_1..z_M` **if and only if** the group algebra
`C[Sym_m x| H]` does, with free part `C[H]`, moves = left multiplication by `[z_t z_t^T]`, target `[I]`.
Here `Sym_m` is the additive group of symmetric m x m matrices over F_2 and H acts by `S -> h S h^T`.

*Proof.* Two algebra homomorphisms, both sending moves to (free unit) x moves and free letters to free letters:
- `psi: C[G_H] -> C[G_H] e_0 = C[Sym_m x| H]`, the projection onto the central sector-0 block (Lemma S). On that
  block every shift is 1, so `dir(z) e_0 = d'_z e_0` and Lemma R(c) identifies `A e_0` with Sym_m, `dir(z) -> zz^T`,
  `K -> I`. A G_H-unit maps to a unit of the same shape.
- `phi: C[Sym_m x| H] -> C[G_H]`, `[S] -> prod d'_{z}` over any decomposition `S = sum zz^T`, `P_h -> P_h`. It is well
  defined because at every character of A (every sector mu, every Q) the product acts as `(-1)^{<Q,S>}` (Lemma R(b)),
  and characters separate C[A]; it respects the semidirect product because `P_h d'_z P_h^-1 = d'_{hz}`. A unit word of
  `C[Sym_m x| H]` maps to a word of `G_H` in which every move `[zz^T]` becomes `c_z dir(z)` (one move, then the free
  element `c_z`), and its output `[I] e_l` becomes `prod_j d'_{e_j} e_l = (prod_j c_{e_j}) K e_l`; one more free
  multiplication by `(prod_j c_{e_j})^-1` gives `K e_l`. Homomorphisms carry formal identities to formal identities. []

Consequences.
1. The no-phase engine game at block m is the group algebra of `Sym_m(F_2) x| GL(m,2)`, which is the parabolic subgroup
   of Sp(2m,2) stabilising the X-type Lagrangian (moves = its root transvections `[[I, zz^T],[0, I]]`, free part = its
   Levi factor). Shifts, signs and the i's of the engine letters play no role in it; only order-2 characters
   (binary quadratic forms) matter. Sizes at block 4: 1024 x 20160, against 16384 x 20160 for `A x| GL`.
2. H = 1 is Corollary R1. So Theorem 17 of ex2-families says exactly: `C[Sym_4]` has no W-in-M unit with M < 4W.
3. The numerical runs `tf.py` / `lm.py` with `--chars 2` and a cyclic H are therefore EXACT formulations of the game
   `G_H` (not relaxations of it); `sec.py` and `--chars 4` are not needed.

## Lemma N (the full engine game: the Pauli-trivial block) [proved]

Let N be the group of Pauli matrices with the scalars of G (`X^a Z^b` times powers of i). N is normal in G (the engine
group), N lies in the free group B (shifts, and `Z^b = (i^{[b.x]})^2`, and `i = i^{[0.x + 1]}` are free letters), and
`G/N = Sp(2m,2)` (at m = 3: |G| = 371,589,120 = 256 x |Sp(6,2)|, |N| = 256). So `e_N = |N|^-1 sum_{n in N} [n]` is a
central idempotent of C[G] lying in C[B], and as in Lemma S every E-unit gives a unit of the same shape in
`C[G] e_N = C[Sp(2m,2)]`, with free part the Siegel parabolic `P_{L0}` (the stabiliser of the Z-type Lagrangian), moves
the transvections `t_z: (x | b) -> (x + (b.z) z | b)`, target the image of K. This is necessary, not sufficient: the
other blocks of C[G] (nontrivial characters of N, which carry the f-dependence of the i's) have no lift like phi.
Theorem A is the special case without phases, where the image of G_H is the X-type parabolic and phi exists.

## Lemma C (channels: one paid move acts as many virtual moves) [proved]

Let H be finite, Q a binary quadratic form, and `V_Q = Ind_{Sym}^{Sym x| H} chi_Q = C^H` (basis |k>, `P_g|k> = |gk>`,
`[S]|k> = (-1)^{<Q, k^-1 S k^-T>}|k>`). Then `[zz^T]` acts as the diagonal `(-1)^{Q(k^-1 z)}` on channel k, `[I]` as
`(-1)^{tr(Q o k^-1)}`, and elements of `C[H]` as left-regular operators. Together over all Q these representations
contain every irreducible representation of `Sym x| H`, so `R_0(H)` is equivalent to: for every Q,

    Atil Pi_Q (I + Gamma Pi_Q)^{-1} Btil = O_Q (x) I_W                                              (**)

with `Atil, Btil, Gamma` (Gamma strictly lower triangular in time order) matrices over `C[H]` acting left-regularly,
`Pi_Q` the projector onto the (move, channel) pairs with `Q(k^-1 z_t) = 1`, `O_Q` the projector onto the channels with
`tr(Q o k^-1) = 1`. (The trivial character forces the free part to be I.) So in channel k the single paid move t
acts as a move in direction `k^-1 z_t`: one paid move is |H| virtual moves in all directions of its H-orbit at once.
What stops this from being free is that the coefficients are only left-regular operators (they cannot project onto one
channel). *Proof.* Mackey: `Ind chi_Q` restricted as stated; the expansion of ex2-families Lemma 1 holds verbatim with
operator coefficients kept in order. []

For a cyclic `H = <sigma>` of order n this is what `tf.py` / `lm.py` solve (`--chars 2`): `n x n` circulant blocks, one Q per
orbit.

## Lemma V (the virtual slice system and its three exact tests) [proved]

Take a cyclic address group `H = <sigma>` of order n and a unit of `C[Sym_m x| H]` with moves `z_1..z_M` (by Theorem
A, the same as a unit of the no-phase game with H). In `Ind chi_Q = C^n` write the transfer data as matrices with
`n x n` circulant blocks: `Atil` (Wn x Mn), `Btil` (Mn x Wn), `Gamma` (Mn x Mn, zero on and above the diagonal blocks).
Call `(t, k)` a virtual move with direction `sigma^-k z_t` and `(l, k)` a virtual array, odd at Q iff
`tr(Q o sigma^-k) = 1`; let `D_Q` be the virtual moves with `Q(sigma^-k z_t) = 1`, `n_odd(Q)` the number of odd
channels, `L = I + Gamma_D`, `E = I + Gamma - Btil Atil`. Then for every binary quadratic form Q:
(a) `Atil_D L^-1 Btil_D = O_Q (x) I_W` (Lemma C), so `|D_Q| >= W n_odd(Q)` (deletion);
(b) `rank E[D_Q, D_Q] = |D_Q| - W n_odd(Q)` (the slack of Q), for odd and even Q alike;
(c) if the slack is 0 ("tight"), `E[D_Q, D_Q] = 0`, so a set Y of virtual moves that lie pairwise in common tight
    supports, each in at least one, has `|Y| <= rank(Btil Atil) <= Wn`;
(d) for every form F and every partition of `supp F x supp F` into rectangles `R x C`,
    `slack(F) <= sum rank E[R, C]`, with `rank E[R, C] <= min(c(R), c(C), slack(G))` for every G whose support
    contains `R u C` (rank cover, as in ex2-families Theorem 17).
*Proof.* (b): the bordered matrix `[[I, Atil_D], [Btil_D, L]]` has Schur complements `I - O_Q (x) I_W` (rank
`Wn - W n_odd`) and `E[D,D]`, so `|D| + Wn - W n_odd = Wn + rank E[D,D]`. (c): with slack 0 the rank of
`O_Q (x) I_W` equals |D|, so `Atil_D` vanishes off the odd rows and `Btil_D` off the odd columns, and on them both
are square and invertible; hence `L = Btil_D Atil_D = K[D, D]` (K = Btil Atil), i.e. `E[D,D] = 0`. K restricted to Y is
then unit lower triangular in time order (virtual moves of one physical move have zero Gamma between them), so
`|Y| <= rank K <= Wn`. (d): `E[D_F, D_F]` is the sum of its rectangles; each rectangle is a submatrix of
`E[D_G, D_G]` for every G containing it, so its rank is at most `slack(G)` by (b). []
These are exactly the slice tests of ex2-families (Lemma 2, Theorem 1, Theorem 17's rank cover) with the target
replaced by a projector; the circulant structure of the coefficients is not used (so the tests are necessary, weaker
than the full system). Programs: `vclique.py` ((a), (c)), `vrc.py` ((b), (d) with all partitions into at most 3 blocks).

## Proposition P (routing alone never saves) [argued]

Fix an irreducible representation of dimension d of the group in question. There a register holds a d x dW matrix
(rows: where the content sits; columns: the W arrays times d input directions), so at most d independent "pieces";
each array brings d pieces, and each piece that is never superposed with another one must climb to the full frame on
its own path, which needs at least m moves (its seen directions must have Gram matrix I, Lemma R(c)). If every piece
is routed this way, one move serves at most d pieces, so M >= m W. A unit at a saving must therefore superpose
pieces in one slot and cancel the cross terms later (the collisions of RULES C1). This is a count over a restricted
class of words, not a bound for all words; it explains why the channel mechanism of Lemma C does not save by itself.
