# Proving that a unit exists without finding it

On 14 and 15 October 2026 I spent three rounds on one question: can I prove that a faster program exists without
writing it down? OpenAI's challenge only asks that such a program exist, and Lean accepts classical proofs, so an
existence proof would count in full. This folder tells what I tried, what held, what broke and what is still open,
with the programs, the Lean files and the working proofs behind each result.

## In short

- **A unit known only to exist counts.** I proved in Lean that the engine of this repository turns a unit given
  only by "there exists" into the kernel program the challenge needs, and that every scalar an OpenAI-model program
  can compute lies in the field generated by the root of unity it is given (only the three standard axioms; re-checked
  for this revision). Any complex unit counts in the end, because its numbers can be rewritten in that field at a
  constant cost (argued by hand, not checked). A claim of the first round that a unit's numbers must come from that
  field was refuted. Any saving above about 2.106e-13 meets the challenge (checked by computer).
- **No route proved that a saving unit exists at any open shape.** Every route was first calibrated on units known
  to exist and on shapes known to be impossible; none produced an existence proof where it matters.
- **Two routes work as certifiers** of fixed mid-size shapes: finite fields (solve modulo a prime, lift, recognise
  the numbers, check exactly) and certified numerics (an interval test plus one exact step). Both need that exact
  step, because a unit that saves sits in special position, where nearby targets have no solution.
- **Several routes are blind, for reasons that are now argued or checked:** counting unknowns against equations (the
  count is wrong even for the plain schedule), limits (a limit that transfers back is the finite problem), Strassen's
  duality (its other side is the unknown exponent), the price reformulation (it calls six impossible shapes savings),
  and rank tests of the engine's matrices (they stop at a constant number of moves per array).
- **What matrix multiplication suggests, half of it carries over.** A mixed unit (one that finishes some arrays only
  partly) is worth exactly the root of its balance equation, the analogue of Schönhage's τ-theorem (argued by hand,
  not checked; two AI skeptics found no error). Combining pieces that respect one common price never beats the best
  single piece (argued by hand, not checked), so the laser method has no direct analogue here.
  The broad reading "every saving must come from one finite unit" was refuted: a piece with a paid block bigger than
  one of its targets can in principle boost another unit. The smallest such pieces are impossible; three larger ones
  survive every test I have.
- **By-products on the impossibility side:** in the commutative slice (the smallest game, with no address maps and no
  phases) the uniform family has no member above the bar at block 6, and every slice unit needs at least 4.65 paid
  moves per array from block 5 on; eight block-4 skeletons have exact impossibility proofs; existence in two games is
  now a finite exact algebra question (and, by a completion theorem of another round, in the full engine game too).
- No person has read any of these proofs. The statements of the two Lean files are proved in Lean; every other
  statement is checked by computer or argued by hand, and its status below says who or what checked it. Most results
  were checked by one AI skeptic, not two.

## The question

A **unit** is a small fixed program that transforms W arrays at once with fewer costly steps than doing them one at
a time. At **block size m** each array must be transformed along m address directions. One **paid move** applies one
direction's 2-point transform to one array (inside the recursion it is one recursive call); everything else is a
**free step**: relabelling addresses, multiplying entries by phases, adding multiples of one array to another. The
plain schedule needs W·m paid moves. A unit "W in M" with M < W·m gives, used recursively, an algorithm with about
N (log N)^(1-s) operations, where the **saving** is s = ln(mW/M)/ln m; the earlier revisions explain how such savings
carry over to the Fourier transform and to convolution. The standing bar for a new route is a saving of 0.1; since 15
October, 0.01 counts as a stepping-stone tier.

My searches up to 14 October were construction: a program proposes the shape of a unit, solves for its numbers, and
the result is checked exactly. Proofs steered the searches by ruling regions out. OpenAI's goal, though, is a
statement that some program exists, and its checker allows Classical.choice. For matrix multiplication, abstract tools
(Schönhage's τ-theorem, the laser method of Coppersmith and Winograd, Strassen's duality) proved that fast algorithms
exist long before anyone wrote them down. So I asked:

1. Does a unit known only to exist count, for OpenAI's challenge and for this repository's Lean pipeline?
2. Can any general tool (counting dimensions, finite fields, certified numerics, limits, duality, a change of
   formulation, families of units at every block size) prove that a unit exists at a shape no search has reached?
3. If not, what must an existence proof look like?

## Words used here

- **Array, block m, paid move, free step, unit "W in M", saving, the bar**: as above.
- **Digit columns f.** Inside the recursion an address splits into f columns of m bits. A unit must be right for every
  f, which is the hardest part of every existence question here.
- **Games.** The *engine game* has the free letters of the Lean engine: shifts, linear address maps and phase letters
  (multiplying by 1 or i by a parity of the address). The *no-phase game* drops the phases. The *commutative slice*
  (or just *slice*) also drops the address maps: paid moves, shifts and mixing only. The *widened game* makes every
  address relabelling and every phase pattern free. The *everything-free game* also allows maps that mix neighbouring
  digit columns. A unit of a smaller game is a unit of every bigger one.
- **Mixed unit.** A unit that finishes some arrays only partly, in k of their m directions. "1 + 4 in 10" at block 3
  finishes 1 array fully and 4 arrays in 2 of their 3 directions with 10 moves.
- **Certificate, hook.** A certificate is my kind of lower bound (Theorem R, proved in Lean; it is told in
  `../block-bounds-and-new-rules/`): a quantity that free steps never raise and each paid move raises by a bounded
  amount. The hook is the best family at block 3.
- **Skeleton, relaxation, stand-in.** A skeleton fixes a candidate's wiring and free steps and leaves its mixing
  numbers unknown. A relaxation is a simpler system every unit of the skeleton must satisfy. A stand-in is a smaller
  problem in which each free letter g acts as g⊗g; its units are not units of the real game.
- **Special position.** A unit that saves is a very special point of its equations: random choices of coefficients
  miss it, and counting arguments do not see it.
- **Calibration.** Before a route may speak about an open shape it must prove that known units exist (the plain
  schedule, the E8 unit of the fourth revision, the block-2 stand-ins, an 8-move control word) and refuse shapes known
  to be impossible (engine 2 in 5 at block 3, a near-miss limit called absorb4 whose residual stays at 2/3).
- **Job model, balance, booster.** A *job* [s] is the transform on s address rows inside a large register. A
  *relation* is an exact program that finishes some jobs with paid calls of stated sizes. Its *balance* at exponent θ
  is the sum of r^θ over its calls minus the sum of k^θ over its finished jobs. A *booster* saves nothing alone but
  has a negative balance at some θ below 1, so that together with a real unit it would lower the exponent.
- **Skeptic.** An AI agent told to refute a result with its own code and its own reading. "One skeptic" means one such
  agent tried and failed; no person and no proof checker has read these proofs unless the status says "proved in
  Lean".

## What I tried

Three rounds, each with one track per route and one skeptic per track; every track wrote its predictions before it
computed, and had to pass the calibration before it could say anything about an open shape.

1. **The existence round** (14 October, morning): what OpenAI's statement accepts, and the Lean check that the engine
   accepts a non-explicit unit; whether counting dimensions can prove existence; whether the τ-theorem and the laser
   method carry over. Two skeptics for the first two tracks; the τ-theorem got its skeptics later, in the recursion
   lever round.
2. **Six routes** (14 October, evening): finite fields, certified numerics, families of units at every block size,
   limits, a change of formulation, and Strassen's duality repaired. One skeptic each.
3. **Large blocks first** (15 October, early morning), because the room for a saving grows with the block: the
   commutative slice at block 6 in both directions, the engine version of the slice's matrix test, the uniform family
   built with address maps, a screen for boosting pieces at blocks 4 to 8, and the block-4 survivor 7 in 23 at the
   exact level. One skeptic each.

## What I found

### 1. What OpenAI's challenge accepts

- **The engine accepts a unit known only to exist** (*proved in Lean*). `ExLegit.lean` has five theorems:
  the kernel program from an existential statement `∃ w, GLiveKernel e w ∧ Proper ∧ price`, by `obtain`, so no word is
  ever written down; the same with the unit named by `Classical.choose`; the price of a mixing letter does not depend
  on its matrix (`rfl`); a fixed shape with unknown coefficients, whose price is proved once for the shape and whose
  correctness is known only for some coefficient vector; and the same from the engine's own existential certificate.
  An AI skeptic rebuilt the file; I re-checked it for this revision on 19 October (only propext, Classical.choice and
  Quot.sound). Two limits are properties of the theorem: the price of a whole shape enters as a hypothesis, proved
  once per shape; and the unit must still be proved correct for every number of digit columns. For a unit known only
  to exist that needs a Lean lemma that does not exist yet ("a formal identity is correct at every f"; my agents
  guessed 500 to 1,500 lines). Mixed units also need a new recursion in Lean, and free steps outside the engine
  (column-mixing maps, the controlled phase, Toffoli) need engine extensions first.
- **Every scalar an OpenAI-model program can compute lies in Q(ζ_d)**, the field generated by the root of unity it
  is given, so its outputs are Q(ζ_d)-linear combinations of its inputs, and every Fourier program has ζ_n in that
  field (*proved in Lean*, `ExLegitClosure.lean`: OpenAI's
  `UniformFourier.lean` unchanged, followed by 252 lines; the only `sorry`s are OpenAI's two open challenge theorems,
  which nothing here uses). An AI skeptic rebuilt it; re-checked for this revision.
- **One fixed program for every length** (*argued by hand, not checked*; two AI skeptics found no error). The program
  must be one finite tree chosen before n. A fixed unit counts however it was found or proved, and so does a fixed
  member of an asymptotic family. "For each length there is some good unit", chosen without a procedure the program
  can run and pay for, does not count. OpenAI's own construction already works by existence: its building blocks are
  stated as "there is code and a constant such that…", and its final program is never written out.
- **Refuted: "a unit's numbers must come from the supplied root's field."** The first round called this the main
  catch. Both skeptics refuted it independently: a solution with any complex numbers gives one with algebraic numbers
  (Hilbert's Nullstellensatz), and storing each value as a short list of coordinates over Q(ζ_d), the way a complex
  number is stored as two reals, costs a constant factor and keeps the exponent. So for the challenge any complex unit
  counts (*argued by hand, not checked*; it is the two skeptics' argument). Inside the engine as it stands any real
  solution counts (a real solution gives a real algebraic one by Tarski's transfer, and the coordinate trick then
  gives rational mixing; *argued by hand, not checked*); complex solutions that are not real need a small engine
  extension. A skeptic's toy turns a unit whose numbers involve the cube root of 2 into one with rational mixing and
  the same ratio of moves to arrays, 2 moves for 1 array becoming 6 for 3 (*checked by computer*; re-run).
- **Any saving above about 2.106e-13 meets the challenge** (*checked by computer*: exact arithmetic on OpenAI's
  constants, 1 − θ = 2.10643843004e-13, recomputed by a skeptic and re-run here). From length 2^20 on, the supplied
  root's field also contains the 8th roots of unity and √2, √3, √5, √13 and √17 (a port of OpenAI's length selection,
  checked on 221 sampled lengths by the track and on 308 by a skeptic, who also showed that it holds for every length
  from 2^20 on; re-run).
- **A test for numerical hits** (*argued by hand, not checked*; upheld by the skeptics). If a numerical solution's
  last mixing layer can still be solved after small real changes to the other layers, a unit with rational mixing
  exists. The proposed fallback test of the first round (discard hits whose numbers have the wrong kind of Galois
  group) was wrong and would have thrown away usable hits.

So the real catch is not the kind of numbers. It is that the existence proof has to exist.

### 2. Counting unknowns and equations cannot prove existence

The most generic existence tool: write a unit as polynomial equations in its unknown numbers; if the unknowns
outnumber the equations and a smooth point exists, solutions exist. Two skeptics checked this track, one in exact
arithmetic modulo two primes.

- **Written exactly, every shape has far more equations than unknowns, the plain schedules included** (*checked by
  computer*, exact counts in the group ring, recounted by a skeptic; re-run). The plain 1 in 2 at block 2 has 18,432
  unknowns against 46,080 equations. So "more equations than unknowns" never means "no solution" here: even the
  ordinary method reaches its target through structure, not because the equations leave room.
- **Neither route from a count to existence is open** (*argued by hand, not checked*; two skeptics). Filling a whole
  neighbourhood of the target would take about 3.657, 14.49 and 65.76 moves per array at blocks 2, 3 and 4, where the
  plain schedule needs 2, 3 and 4; and the problem's symmetries are too few to move the target to a typical point.
- **Where counts can be computed, the savings sit in special position** (*checked by computer*; both skeptics
  reproduced the numbers with their own code). In the block-2 stand-in the program's outputs fill an open set exactly
  at plain cost and never below it, yet 2 in 3 and 3 in 5 have exact solutions there; a count at random points would
  have called them impossible.
- **The stand-ins that can be computed at blocks 3 and 4 are blind**: even the impossible 1 in 2 at block 3 is
  solvable in them (*checked by computer*).
- **The everything-free game has no finite count at all** (*argued by hand, not checked*, given an irreducibility
  result that holds at every f at block 2 and was checked at f = 3 at block 3): at each fixed number of digit columns
  its free steps already produce every matrix, so all the content is in "one word right for every f".
- A by-product, an orbit test (a target whose symmetry orbit is bigger than the program's image cannot be reached),
  excluded nothing that Theorem R does not.
- The skeptics' correction, which stays **open**: what is proved is that counting in the whole space cannot prove
  existence. Whether a count could exclude a shape, and counts inside a smaller natural space such as a certificate's
  rank locus, are not covered.

### 3. The matrix-multiplication route: the τ-theorem holds, the laser half does not

- **The τ-theorem transfers exactly** (*argued by hand, not checked*; two AI skeptics found no error when they checked
  its extension to grouped paid blocks in the recursion-lever round). A mixed unit with W_k arrays owed k directions
  and M paid moves is worth exactly the exponent τ that solves Σ W_k·k^τ = M. The proof splits the top job once,
  feeds units at many sizes in a balanced flow, and runs their moves in lockstep, because a careless order deadlocks.
  The flow balances exactly at small sizes, and for 1 + 4 in 10 the constant of the induction is at most 1.2132 over
  sampled sizes up to n = 10^7 (*checked by computer*, exact fractions; re-run). It is a theorem in a job model and is
  not in Lean as a general theorem; what the record builds prove in Lean is told in `../record-track/`. On the shapes
  it was meant for, 1 + 4 in 10 at block 3 would be worth 0.119 and 7 + 38 in 88 would be worth 0.125; both were later
  excluded by other rounds: in the engine game by Theorem M, a certificate from the group Sp(6, 2)
  (`../theory-of-theories-and-logic/`), and in the engine and widened games by Theorem T on units tight on the hook
  (`../moonshot-small-blocks/`).
- **Using a mixed unit inside today's engine loses a little** (*checked by computer*: a linear program, a price, not
  a proof; re-run). Packing it into one big word at block B loses about 0.09/ln B; at B = 96 the savings are 0.099 and
  0.105 instead of 0.119 and 0.125. The full value needs the τ-theorem's schedule as a new recursion.
- **Combining pieces that respect one price never beats the best single piece** (Theorem 3, the Φ barrier; *argued by
  hand, not checked*: no skeptic has checked it; the duality track of the second round found it correct as stated, and
  only its broad corollary below fails). Powers drift towards no saving: for 1 + 4 in 10 the exponent goes 0.881,
  0.936, 0.950, 0.960, 0.967, 0.971 over 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 12 factors (*checked by computer*; re-run). In matrix
  multiplication a power multiplies cost and value and unwanted pieces can be zeroed out; here the product that
  multiplies sizes acts inside a dense transform, where nothing can be zeroed.
- **Refuted: "so any saving must come from one finite unit."** The first round drew this conclusion from Theorem 3.
  The duality track of the second round showed that it needs every paid block to be no bigger than every target: a
  piece with a bigger paid block can boost another unit's saving, and its skeptic reproduced the effect on made-up
  rules (the combination has exponent 0.723 against 0.834 for the unit alone; lower is better). Whether any real piece
  of this game boosts is section 9.
- **The truncation lemma** (Lemma 6; *argued by hand, not checked*; no skeptic of its own). If a unit begins or ends
  some array with a block on that array alone, dropping the block and treating the array as mixed always raises the
  unit's value. Its first large what-ifs on community circuits failed, because fewer blocks than assumed sit at the
  start of their arrays. Together with the τ-theorem it became the recursion lever of the record track; how, and what
  of it is proved in Lean, is told in `../record-track/`.

### 4. Finite fields

A solution modulo a prime is cheap to find. When does it prove that a complex unit exists? One skeptic checked every
claim of this track with its own parser and exact arithmetic.

- **One prime never decides existence, in either direction** (*argued by hand, not checked*; elementary, with the
  skeptic's agreement). The system x − 1 = 0, x² − 1 − p = 0 has a clean solution modulo p and none over the complex
  numbers; p·x = 1 is the reverse. My systems always have surplus equations, exactly where Hensel's lifting lemma
  breaks down.
- **Three ways from finite fields to a complex unit do work** (*argued by hand, not checked*; the skeptic checked them
  line by line). (a) Find a solution modulo p, lift it p-adically, recognise its numbers, and check every equation
  exactly; only the exact check carries proof, so a failed search can never produce a false "exists". (b) One prime
  plus an identity with integer coefficients, which amounts to smoothness over the p-adic integers. (c) Solutions
  modulo many primes with an effective Nullstellensatz (Krick, Pardo and Sombra): a genuine existence proof that never
  computes the solution, but it needs 2,636 primes at 3 unknowns, about 500,000 at 5 and about 10^13 at 15, so toy
  sizes only (*checked by computer*: one plain chart with 3 unknowns was shown to exist this way, with solutions at
  2,636 consecutive primes above 2^30, not re-run; the table of counts was re-run).
- **Calibration passed** within the limits the track declared: the plain schedule at block 4 as one joint unit, plain
  cost relaxations, a special-position control solvable only on the plane a4 = 3, and two relaxations whose solutions
  need i and a cube root of unity were all certified; engine 2 in 5, the absorb4 limit and other excluded skeletons
  were refused. The E8 unit and the control word were not run, because they are explicit exact words with no system to
  solve; the route is blind on the stand-ins (1,672 unknowns, with the special point known only numerically).
- **All 17 hits of a relaxation screen of block-4 skeletons are feasible over the complex numbers**: 15 over the
  rationals, one over Q(i), one over Q(√−3) (*checked by computer*: the skeptic checked all 27 exact points the track
  claims with its own code; re-run). So that relaxation can exclude none of them. That the two irrational ones need i
  or a cube root of unity is evidence from which primes carry solutions, not proof.
- **Eight block-4 skeletons have exact impossibility proofs** (*checked by computer*: exact identities 1 = Σ g_i·f_i
  over the rationals for every one of the 122 exclusion steps (one of them is a system that reduces to the constant
  −1), and for 75 steps of the two hardest skeletons in a partial snapshot; the skeptic re-checked all 240 certificate
  outputs and rebuilt the systems in exact arithmetic, 160 of 162 identical and the other 2 certified separately;
  re-run). They read x·x correctly as x², so they stand independently of the msolve 0.6.5 bug, which misread such
  terms. They hold for the exported systems; they assume the closure model, a relaxation lemma and a split into charts
  that are told in `../theory-of-theories-and-logic/`.
- **Refuted, a side claim:** "random search never finds the a4 = 3 control" came from a search box that could not
  contain 3 (the skeptic).

What blocks it: existence from finite fields always needs an input from characteristic 0, an exact point or an integer
identity, and locating a special point needs a Gröbner basis modulo p, out of reach at thousands of unknowns. It is a
certifier and a locator for mid-size skeletons, not a tool for large blocks.

### 5. Certified numerics, and the first exact pass on an object in special position

- **In the block-2 stand-in, W arrays can share s paid moves exactly when s ≥ ⌈3W/2⌉** (Theorem 9; *argued by hand,
  not checked*, and *checked by computer*: one skeptic checked the "only if" argument line by line and rebuilt the
  data with its own code; the stored schemes for 2 in 3 and 3 in 5 pass two independent exact checkers, and I re-ran
  both checkers on 19 October: PASS). So 2 in 3, 3 in 5 and 4 in 6 exist exactly there, with Gaussian-rational
  numbers. This is the first calibration pass on an object in special position. The stand-in is not the real game, and
  these are not units of it.
- **Numerics alone certify only stable points** (*argued by hand, not checked*): an interval test proves existence
  only where every nearby target is solvable too, and every unit at a saving is unstable (argued). So an exact step is
  always needed.
- **Relaxations with no repeated irreducible piece are blind to depth** (*argued by hand, not checked*, under a
  stated condition). Two pieces of the geometric-complexity relaxations (21 and 27 dimensions) admit W arrays in 2W
  moves at block 3, and a 35-dimensional piece admits 6 in 7, while engine units need at least 2.8 moves per array
  (*checked by computer*; the skeptic reproduced the block ranks in two models of its own). Existence in such a
  relaxation is no evidence for a unit. The skeptic corrected two summary sentences: "such relaxations have depth 1"
  is false for one 35-dimensional piece, and "building from the inputs alone fails with three or more pieces"
  overstates (one three-piece case reaches 3 in 7 that way, numerically).
- **In bulk, 628 of 746 systems that Singular calls empty got exact impossibility certificates, and none of the 15
  solvable ones was refused** (*checked by computer*; the skeptic re-derived all 571 degree-0 and all 71 degree-1
  certificate records exactly over Q(i) with its own code and found none for a solvable system; the single degree-2
  certificate was not re-derived).
- **Calibration** held as reported: all 17 solvable plain-cost systems certified; all 6 skeletons of engine 2 in 5, an
  infeasible plain skeleton and a border 2 in 7 refused. The skeptic's caveat: the interval method itself is still
  blind on the stand-in; the pass there came from structure.

The closest object: a 56-dimensional relaxation at block 3 in which 5 in 14 sits exactly on its capacity bound, only
numerically, and below the bar.

### 6. Families of units at every block size, and the commutative slice

- **Theorem 10**: any slice program yields one M × M matrix E, nonsingular on the support of every even quadratic form
  and with rank equal to the slack on every odd one (*argued by hand, not checked*; one skeptic re-derived it and
  tested it on genuine sharing units of its own).
- **The slice never saves at block 4: M ≥ 4W** (Theorem 17; *checked by computer*: an exact linear-programming
  certificate, plus a hand proof; one skeptic rebuilt the program and checked the hand proof; re-run).
- **At block 5 every slice unit needed at least 155/36 = 4.306 moves per array** (Theorem 18; *checked by computer*;
  re-run). Superseded the next morning by 4.65 (section 10).
- **The uniform family U_m** (one move in each of the 2^m − 1 directions, shared by up to 2^(m−2) arrays; it comes
  from Reed-Muller codes and would save close to 1 at a constant sharing fraction) was excluded in the slice within 10
  % of full sharing at every block (*argued by hand, not checked*; one skeptic). Superseded by the star lemma (section
  10).
- **What a family can give** (*argued by hand*, elementary): if moves per array grow like m^d, the saving tends to
  1 − d, and a family grown by a fixed gadget is worth no more than its first member. Only families with a bounded
  number of moves per array matter.
- The method's ceiling (*argued by hand*): its inequalities give only about 4.4 moves per array, so they cannot settle
  the bar from block 6 on, and outside the slice they see nothing.

### 7. Limits

- **Infinitely many digit columns, in the engine and widened games: the limit is the finite problem itself**, for the
  f in one residue class modulo 4 (*argued by hand, not checked*; the skeptic supplied this corrected reason: the
  engine letters contain i, so one limit sees one central character).
- **In games that couple columns**, a program wrong on a vanishing share of inputs cannot count, for combinations of
  words with at most one paid letter, and for words of Clifford letters only (*argued by hand, not checked*; a
  Reed-Muller minimum-distance argument; exact computations up to 8 columns agree).
- **Infinitely many address bits**: restricting to the first n bits maps a limit unit back to block n, so no limit
  unit with finitely many moves exists; one with infinitely many moves is a family, worth no more than its best member
  (*argued by hand, not checked*).
- The pattern (*argued by hand*): a limit that transfers back is the finite problem, and a softer one accepts objects
  that do not transfer.
- Corrected by the skeptic: the greedy 14-move program of another round (`../block-bounds-and-new-rules/`) is not a
  unit at any f; what passes at 5 columns and fails at 6 is a certificate's tightness.

### 8. Changing the formulation: OpenAI's prices

- **Refuted: "OpenAI's price mechanism can prove a saving of at most 0.036"** (the track's Proposition 3). Its
  hypothesis is inconsistent at every exponent below 1: OpenAI's direct-sum law and the assumed homogeneity give one
  matrix two different prices (the skeptic; *checked by computer*, re-run). The 0.036, the "35 % cheaper pair" and the
  "gap factor of about 1.9" are void.
- **The price language is blind** (*checked by computer*, the skeptic): of the 14 shapes it "reproduces", 6 are
  proved impossible (2 in 5 at block 3 in three groupings, 3 in 8 in two, and 1 + 4 in 10 in the engine game).
- **Proposition 1**: a saving above 0.1 exists exactly when no "0.9-price" exists (*open*: argued, and its key step,
  building a program from a saving mixture of rules with several kernels, is not written out).
- "Right for every f" is the Burnside-Brauer theorem in this language (*argued by hand*; the skeptic agreed).

### 9. Strassen's duality, repaired, and boosting pieces

- **The repair**: in the real recursion every array sits in a register with spare address bits, and there one
  direction costs two big calls plus free steps (*checked by computer*, exactly for blocks 1 to 6 by the skeptic;
  re-run). With that, Strassen's duality theorem (in Fritz's form) applies as stated.
- **It cannot create a saving** (*argued by hand, not checked*; one skeptic, who also checked the citations): its dual
  side has one parameter, the exponent, and that is the unknown. To apply it at the bar one must already hold units
  that beat every exponent from 0.9 to 1.
- **A relation whose balance changes sign once is worth its root** (*argued by hand, not checked*; one skeptic): so
  mixed units and units with grouped paid blocks reach their full value without an explicit schedule. Not checked by
  anyone: that the job model's exponent equals the challenge's (argued in the τ-theorem track).
- **The simplest boosting pieces are impossible** (*argued by hand, not checked*; one skeptic): an older symmetric
  certificate charges a block of rank d in proportion to 1 − 2^(−d), and that rules out the "spreading" piece 2[2] ≤
  [1] + [3] (two arrays owed 2 of 3 directions, served by a one-direction and a three-direction block) and every piece
  like it, at every block.
- **No booster whose jobs all have at most 3 rows exists, on any register, and such relations never amplify one
  another** (Theorem B1 and Corollary B2 of the third round; *argued by hand, not checked*; one skeptic redid the
  identities and both Wronskians).
- **Refuted as stated: "any booster needs a job of at least 6 rows."** It holds only on registers of at most 16 rows,
  and only given projection certificates that are argued, not proved (the skeptic). What the screen does cover: jobs
  of at most 4 rows on registers of 17, 24 or 32 rows, and registers of at most 4 rows (*checked by computer*,
  rigorous).
- **Three boosters, S6, S7 and S8, pass every certificate I have** (*checked by computer*, about 65,000 of the
  skeptic's own tests each; *open*). S7 is [2] + [5] + [6] ≤ 2[3] + [7] at block 7 and would give a saving of at least
  0.133; S6 is [2] + 4[5] ≤ 2[3] + [4] + 2[6] (at least 0.319); S8 is [2] + [5] + 2[6] ≤ [3] + 2[4] + [8] (at least
  0.242). The skeptic's Lemma L (*argued by hand, not checked by anyone else*) says that the last paid block of an
  exactly frame-tight program has a target's rank, which rules out these three in the form listed; forms with a
  catalyst or with slack stay open.
- On two community words the job model's root agrees with this repository's pricing tools to every printed digit
  (1.40400794216e-3 and 1.67498901594e-3; *checked by computer*). For pure units both solve the same equation, so this
  checks the data, not the general claim.

### 10. Large blocks first

- **The star lemma: in the slice, 13 in 63 at block 6 is impossible** (Theorem C; *argued by hand, not checked*; one
  skeptic checked every step and tested the lemma exactly over four fields, 1,432 cases, no violation; the computer
  checks re-run). No nonsingular 16 × 16 matrix indexed by the 4-dimensional affine space over F_2 has rank at most 3
  on all 30 of its hyperplanes, and a 13-in-63 unit would force exactly that pattern. With 14 to 16 in 63 already
  excluded and 12 in 63 saving less than 0.1, the uniform family has no member above the bar at block 6.
- **Repeating the star lemma excludes U_m from about 0.81 to 0.82 of full sharing at every block** (Theorem D; *argued
  by hand, not checked*; one skeptic; numbers checked by computer). At block 7 this excludes 26 to 28 in 127; 23 to 25
  in 127 stay open (savings 0.122 to 0.165). At block 8, 52 or more of 64 are excluded and 40 to 51 stay open above
  the bar. This local argument can never go below 3/4 of full sharing: matrices built from bent functions meet its
  pattern exactly at even blocks, and the skeptic supplied matching ones at every odd block.
- **Every slice unit from block 5 on needs at least 93/20 = 4.65 moves per array** (Theorem E; *checked by computer*:
  an exact certificate, re-verified by two independent programs; one skeptic; re-run). The slice saves at most 0.045
  at block 5; at block 6 the bar allows 5.02 moves per array, so the slice could still save up to 0.142 there.
- **Existence in two games is now a finite exact algebra question** (*argued by hand, not checked*; one skeptic each).
  In the slice, a program exists exactly when the equations at all binary quadratic forms (the order-2 system) have a
  solution; the proof inserts the free correction ((1 − i) + (1 + i)·X^z)/2 after each move, and the skeptic built the
  corrected 2 in 7 at register level, right at all 512 characters and on random arrays with 1 to 6 digit columns.
  Without phase letters, the engine game with address group H has a unit "W in M" exactly when the group algebra
  C[Sym_m ⋊ H] has one (Sym_m: the symmetric m × m binary matrices; a move multiplies by z·zᵀ; the target is the
  identity). With phases, every engine unit gives a unit of the group algebra of Sp(2m, 2), a necessary condition only
  in this round; a round of the same day proved the converse (every program of this "frame game" completes to an
  engine unit, with one scratch array; argued by hand, one skeptic for each half; `../frame-packing-two-arrays/`).
  An unproved reduction that earlier slice statements relied on is no longer needed. At block 6 the slice system has
  about 3,600 unknowns and 3.5 × 10^8 equations, out of reach.
- **The engine version of the slice's matrix test stops at a constant** (*argued by hand, not checked*, with exact
  computations; one skeptic rebuilt every letter as an actual matrix). As a rank test it is blind, because the target
  is conjugate to a free phase letter, so a word with zero moves passes. Its pinned version proves
  M ≥ max(2W, W + m − 1), which reproduces "block 2 never saves" but does not refuse engine 2 in 5 at block 3, and
  solutions found by search show it cannot prove more than 2 moves per array at block 3 or 2.5 at block 4. The honest
  label is "the refusal half of the calibration failed; no hit". So bounds of this matrix kind cannot close the large
  blocks; only an existence proof or a new kind of bound can.
- **The uniform family built with address maps: no unit, and not excluded at block 5.** The searches found no 4 in 15
  and no 8 in 31. **Refuted in part:** the track's claim that address maps make the slice's exact tests blind. The
  skeptic showed that the tests depend on how the address group sits relative to the target: tight cliques still
  exclude 4 in 15 for 36 of the 56 conjugates of the track's order-3 group, 48 of the 336 conjugates of its order-5
  group and every coordinate-permutation group tried; only the Singer groups of order 15 escape in every conjugate
  (*checked by computer*, certificate re-checked pair by pair). At block 5, 8 in 31 (saving 0.158) and 7 in 31 pass
  every test computed, with the Singer cycle of order 31 and 300 random conjugates of it (*checked by computer*;
  *open*). Later rounds took this family further: in its natural form at block 5, 9 and 10 in 31 are impossible and 8
  in 31 is down to 127 classes of targets (`../reed-muller-and-one-move/`).
- **The block-4 survivor 7 in 23 (saving 0.142): "excluded" was refuted** by its skeptic: random move contents fail
  the same test in exactly the same way, so the exclusion applied to one generic realisation, not to the survivor. A
  by-product holds (*checked by computer*; re-run): the f = 2 widened test with unknown move contents is blind, since
  it accepts 1 array in 2 moves at block 4, where 4 are needed. A later wave closed this construction in both move
  orders in the configuration that was built; its nearest variant, 7 in 24, is open (`../moonshot-block4-block5/`).

## Which routes are alive

| route | calibration, as its skeptic judged | can it prove that a unit exists? | reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| finite fields | passes | yes, on a fixed shape, through an exact check | mid-size; not thousands of unknowns |
| certified numerics | passes (the stand-in through structure) | yes, at stable points or with an exact step | mid-size; a few thousand unknowns at most |
| exact algebra in the slice and the no-phase game | not a route of its own; the reductions of section 10 | yes, if a solution of the finite system is found | small blocks only: about 30,000 unknowns for the no-phase system at block 5, and 3,600 unknowns with 3.5 × 10^8 equations for the slice at block 6, both beyond the solvers |
| families | passes in the slice only | none found; an exclusion tool | every block, slice only |
| counting dimensions | the count is wrong even for the plain schedule | no | — |
| limits | passes in substance | no: limits that transfer are the finite problem | — |
| reformulation (prices) | weak form only | not yet: a target without a mechanism | — |
| duality | blind both ways | no: circular | — |
| matrix tests in the engine | the refusal half fails | no: stops at a constant per array | — |
| τ-theorem and laser | — | the τ half prices mixed units exactly; the laser half gives nothing for pieces that respect one price | job model |

What an existence proof must now produce: one finite unit or relation (or a booster with an oversized paid block),
exact or formally polynomial, with any complex numbers, in special position; so the proof must use structure or exact
algebra on one fixed shape, as Strassen's identity and the laser method's degenerations did for matrix multiplication.

## What failed, in one list

These claims broke; each is told in context above.

- "A unit's numbers must come from the supplied root's field": refuted by both skeptics of the first round.
- The first round's fallback test for numerical hits (discard hits with the wrong kind of Galois group): wrong.
- "Any saving must come from one finite unit": refuted by the duality track (boosting by an oversized paid block).
- "OpenAI's price mechanism is capped at a saving of 0.036": refuted (its hypothesis is inconsistent below exponent
  1).
- "Random search never finds the a4 = 3 control": an artefact of the search box.
- "Any booster needs a job of at least 6 rows": refuted as stated (it holds only on registers of at most 16 rows,
  given argued projections).
- "Address maps make the slice's exact tests blind": refuted in part (it depends on the placement of the group).
- "The 7-in-23 survivor is excluded": refuted (the test fails on random contents in the same way).
- The engine matrix test's label "calibrated, no hit": generous; its refusal half failed.
- Smaller slips that the skeptics corrected: two wrong calibration reasons of the limits track; "448 characters"
  (it is 256) for the uncorrected 2 in 7; "relaxations with no repeated piece have depth 1" (false for one piece);
  "inputs-only fails with three or more pieces" (overstated).
- The first large what-ifs of the truncation lemma on community circuits (told in `../record-track/`).

## How this fits the rest of the research

The existence routes changed how the other rounds searched. Because a unit that saves sits in special position, later
rounds dropped random-coefficient searches for tight shapes and pushed every candidate through an exact check; finite
fields and certified numerics became the standard last step. The slice and no-phase reductions of section 10 are the
starting point of the Reed-Muller rounds (`../reed-muller-and-one-move/`). The τ-theorem and the truncation lemma
became the recursion lever (`../record-track/`). The impossibility results that the mixed targets of section 3 ran
into are in `../theory-of-theories-and-logic/` (Theorem M) and `../moonshot-small-blocks/` (Theorem T). The completion
of the frame game, which makes the full engine game a group-algebra question as well, is in
`../frame-packing-two-arrays/`. The first round ranked the everything-free game at block 2 (2 in 3, saving 0.415) as
the best place for an existence proof by exact algebra; it was closed the next day from the other side, by Theorem 2W
(block 2 never saves in any game with bounded free steps; argued by hand, one skeptic; `../moonshot-small-blocks/`).
