# sl-algebra: the crossing-slack accounting in the group algebra (proofs)

15 October 2026, 10:10 to about 11:10 EDT (Mac clock). One agent; no skeptic has read this. Labels: [proved] (proof below),
[computed] (script in `calc/` and its output), [read] (from a named file, not re-derived), [argued].
Moves are counted one per direction (the brief's M). Δ := W·m − M (the move deficit).

## 0. Setting: a unit is an identity in a group algebra

- **Letters.** P is the engine's free group: Paulis, invertible affine address maps, diagonal Clifford phases
  i^{q(x)} (q a Z_4-valued quadratic form), and the scalars i^k. C = ½[[1+i, 1−i], [1−i, 1+i]] on one bit is the
  paid kernel (it is e^{iπ/4}·S⁻¹HS⁻¹, an-openai §1). A paid move is left multiplication by any element of
  P·C_1·P (a "directional kernel"). G is the finite group generated by P and C_1. The target kernel is T = C^{⊗m}
  (or H^{⊗m}; they differ by free letters up to scalars).
- **Unit.** W arrays. The state is a W×W matrix A(t) with entries in the group algebra C[G]; entry (r, s) is
  what array r holds of input s. Steps: a *gate* A ← B·A with B ∈ GL_W(C) (pointwise mixing of arrays); a *free
  step* on array r (row r ← p·row r, p ∈ P); a *paid move* on array r (row r ← h·row r, h a directional kernel).
  A(0) = I and A(end) = D·T with D monomial with entries in C^×·P (free relabelling and corrections; every array,
  scratch included, gets the transform). M = the number of paid moves.
- **Fact 0** [proved, short]. A word is correct at every number f of digit columns iff the identity holds in
  C[G]. One direction is trivial. Conversely the f-column action is g ↦ g^{⊗f} on V^{⊗f}, V = C^{2^m}, which is
  faithful for G; by Burnside–Brauer every irreducible of G occurs in some V^{⊗f}, f ≥ 0, and in fact for some
  f ≥ 1 (if U ⊂ V is irreducible and U* ⊂ V^{⊗f'}, then the trivial module sits in V^{⊗(1+f')}). So ⊕_{f≥1} V^{⊗f}
  is a faithful C[G]-module. This is the model every exact checker of ours uses ("formal identity"); in it, the
  *terms* of a content are well defined, because group elements are a basis of C[G].

## 1. Frames

**Definition 1.** G acts on the Paulis by conjugation, which gives G → Sp(2m, 2). Let L_0 = {(0|z)} (the
Z-type Lagrangian of F_2^{2m}) and, for g ∈ G, **the frame of g**: λ(g) = g⁻¹·L_0. Frames are the vertices of the
dual polar graph DSp(2m, 2) (15, 135, 2,295 frames at m = 2, 3, 4); d(A, B) = m − dim(A ∩ B).

**Lemma 1** [proved; computed `calc/frames.py` (a), m ≤ 3].
- (a) λ(p·g) = λ(g) for every free p (P fixes L_0; it is the full preimage of the stabiliser of L_0, as the
  lagrangian dictionary records [read: `../../lagrangian/NOTE.md`, dictionary]).
- (b) d(λ(h·g), λ(g)) = 1 for every directional kernel h (λ(hg) = g⁻¹·(h⁻¹L_0), and h⁻¹L_0 is a neighbour of L_0).
- (c) λ(g·k) = k⁻¹·λ(g): right multiplication is an isometry of the graph.
- (d) λ(e) = L_0, λ(C^{⊗m}) = L_Y = {(z|z)} and λ(H^{⊗m}) = L_X = {(x|0)}, both at distance m from L_0.
Write L_T := λ(T).

**Subspace frames** [computed, `calc/frames.py` (b)–(d), every subspace, m ≤ 4]. For any subspace U ⊆ F_2^m,
L_U = {(x | x + w) : x ∈ U, w ∈ U^⊥} is Lagrangian; d(L_U, L_V) = dim(U+V) − dim(U∩V); for nondegenerate U,
L_U = λ(Φ_U) (OpenAI's frame Φ_U = J·diag(i^{|P_U x|})·J), L_U and L_{U^⊥} are opposite, and
Φ_{U^⊥}Φ_U⁻¹ = C^{⊗m}·(a Pauli). For degenerate U, L_U and L_{U^⊥} are not opposite.

## 2. Common-frame networks and the exact accounting

**Definition 2 (common-frame network).** A unit is a *common-frame network* if there are **twists** k_1, …, k_W ∈ G
(one per input) such that at every time t every row of A(t)·diag(k_1, …, k_W) has the form (scalar row b_r(t)) ×
(one group element g_r(t)). Its **frame path** is Λ(r, t) = λ(g_r(t)); its **source frame** a_s = λ(k_s) =
k_s⁻¹L_0, and the **sink frame of input s** is a*_s = k_s⁻¹L_T (opposite to a_s, by Lemma 1(c)).

Consequences of the definition [proved]: a non-monomial gate only joins rows with the same element (otherwise an
output row would carry two elements), i.e. arrays at a common frame: this is the hub's equal-frame rule and
OpenAI's "gates commute with a common frame". With k_s = Φ_{U_s} the source frames are OpenAI's labels L_{U_s}.
If array r ends holding input σ(r), its last frame is e_r = λ(p T k_{σ(r)}) = a*_{σ(r)}.

**Theorem 1 (exact accounting)** [proved]. In every common-frame network, with len_r the number of paid moves on
array r and exc_r = len_r − d(a_r, e_r) ≥ 0:

  M = Σ_r len_r,   Δ = Σ_r dim(a_r ∩ e_r) − Σ_r exc_r,   e_r = a*_{σ(r)}.

For every reference frame o, with N_o = Σ_s d(o, a_s), L_o = ½·Σ_r [len_r − d(o, e_r) + d(o, a_r)] ≥ 0 and
E_o = Σ_s [d(o, a_s) + d(o, a*_s) − m] ≥ 0:

  M = W·m − 2N_o + 2L_o + E_o,   hence M ≥ W·m − 2N_o + 2L_o,

with equality iff o lies on a geodesic from a_s to a*_s for every s.

*Proof.* Free steps and gates do not move Λ (Lemma 1(a); a non-monomial gate keeps the common element; a monomial
gate is a relabelling and the content keeps its path), and each paid move moves it by exactly 1 (Lemma 1(b)).
So len_r is the length of array r's path from Λ(r,0) = λ(k_r) = a_r to e_r, and M = Σ len_r. Then
m − len_r = (m − d(a_r, e_r)) − exc_r = dim(a_r ∩ e_r) − exc_r. For the second form,
len_r = d(o, e_r) − d(o, a_r) + 2·(½(len_r − d(o,e_r) + d(o,a_r))), and the second bracket is ≥ 0 by the
triangle inequality. Summing, and using that r ↦ σ(r) is a bijection, Σ_r d(o, e_r) = Σ_s d(o, a*_s)
= Σ_s (m − d(o, a_s)) + E_o. ∎

**OpenAI's identity is the case** o = L_0, subspace frames, nondegenerate source labels U_s and sinks U_s^⊥: then
d(L_0, L_U) = dim U (so N_o = Σ dim U_s), E_o = 0, and a move between subspace frames changes dim by exactly ±1,
so L_o = the total dimension dropped on descents. That is M = W·m − 2N + 2L [computed: frames.py (b)–(c); and
an-openai's brute-force check, re-run here at h = 7: "equal: True", `calc/rerun/labels7.out`].

**Corollary 1** [proved]. (i) An array that ends with its own input (e_r = a*_r) has dim(a_r ∩ e_r) = 0: it saves
nothing. (ii) A network whose gates are all monomial (pure routing) has M ≥ W·m: every content path then carries one
input from a_s to a*_s.

**Proposition 2 (what the "source dimension" means)** [proved].
- The twists of a common-frame network are fixed only up to one common right factor z (k_s ↦ k_s z keeps every
  row single-term), which moves every frame by the same isometry z⁻¹. So the split of Δ into "2N − 2L" depends on
  a reference frame o; only Δ and the overlaps dim(a_r ∩ e_r) are intrinsic.
- Example: the plain schedule with every twist k_s = T⁻¹ has a_s = L_T, N_{L_0} = W·m and L_{L_0} = W·m; with
  k_s = e it has N = L = 0. Same unit.
- What is intrinsic is the **median bound** Δ ≤ 2·min_o Σ_s d(o, a_s), and likewise with the a*_s (Theorem 1 with
  the best o, and its time reverse). So the anatomy's ceiling "Δ/(W·m) ≤ 2·(average source dimension)/m" is a
  true statement for every common-frame network once "source dimension" is read as distance to the best o. It
  caps designs whose source frames cluster near one frame — every known design: lines, distance 1 from L_0. It
  does not cap common-frame networks whose sources are spread over the graph; for those the cost is in the
  bridges, and no general lower bound on bridges is known.

**Proposition 3 (switching)** [proved]. Cut every frame path at the gates. At a non-monomial gate (all its arrays
at one frame) the incoming and outgoing segments may be re-matched by any bijection. For every re-matching,
M ≥ Σ (distance between the two ends of each re-matched path).

**Corollary 2 (two arrays never save in a common-frame network)** [proved]. If W = 2 (scratch included), every
common-frame network has M ≥ 2m, at every block and for every choice of twists.
*Proof.* With no non-monomial gate, Corollary 1(ii). Otherwise both arrays meet at a common frame at least once,
so both pairings of starts with ends are available (re-match at that gate, keep the others). Take the pairing
that joins a_r to the end of the path that finishes with input r, i.e. to a*_r. By Proposition 3,
M ≥ d(a_1, a*_1) + d(a_2, a*_2) = 2m. ∎
This is an-openai §2.3's lattice argument, made general. **Consequence:** every two-array target still open can
only exist outside the common-frame class: 2 in 7 at block 4 (0.096); 2 in 7 (0.222), 2 in 8 (0.139) at block 5;
2 in 8 (0.226), 2 in 9 (0.161), 2 in 10 (0.102) at block 6 (MAP §3.3–3.4, with mw-big56's two-layer bound
M ≥ W + m, unchecked, which removes 2 in 6 at block 5 and 2 in 7 at block 6).

**Corollary 2′ (two arrays with single-term contents never save)** [proved]. If W = 2 and every entry of A(t) is
always a single term c·g (b4-depth4's "family S": at most one term per input in every content), then M ≥ 2m, at
every block. No common frame is assumed.
*Proof.* Cut time into *separate periods* (each array holds one input; then only monomial gates are possible, since
a non-monomial gate on (g₁, 0) and (0, g₂) makes a row with both inputs) and *joint episodes* (some array holds
both). In a joint episode every array holding both inputs has the same twist τ = g₁⁻¹g₂: an episode opens with a
merge of (g₁, 0) and (0, g₂), and every later gate must match the elements of the inputs the rows share (a sum of
two different elements is two terms). So inside the episode the twists (e, τ⁻¹) make it a common-frame network,
and every non-monomial gate in it is at a common frame. Proposition 3 inside the episode, with the pairing that
joins the array holding input s at the episode's start to the one holding s at its end (available because the
episode has a non-monomial gate), gives: moves in the episode ≥ (distance input 1's frame travels) + (distance
input 2's frame travels), measured in raw frames at the two boundaries (τ is an isometry). In a separate period
the same holds trivially. Summing, and by the triangle inequality from L_0 to L_T for each input, M ≥ 2m. ∎
This turns b4-depth4's certificate-checked "family S: two arrays need 2m" (proofs NOTE) into a short proof, for
every block. **So every open two-array target needs, at some moment, an array holding two different group elements
of the same input** (several terms per input), not just additions between different frames.

**Proposition 4 (cut lemma)** [proved]. In a common-frame network let V_λ(t) ⊆ C^W be the span of the scalar rows
b_r(t) of the arrays at frame λ. Then C^W = ⊕_λ V_λ(t); gates keep every V_λ; a paid move takes one vector out of
V_λ and into V_μ (μ a neighbour). For a set S of frames, V_S = ⊕_{λ∈S} V_λ therefore changes only when a move
crosses the boundary of S, by one dimension each time, so the number of crossing moves is at least the subspace
distance dim(V_S(0) + V_S(end)) − dim(V_S(0) ∩ V_S(end)) = #{s : exactly one of a_s, a*_s lies in S}.
- Concentric cuts {λ : d(o, λ) < i}, i = 1..m, give back the median bound of Proposition 2 (each move crosses at
  most one of them). So for designs whose sources cluster, cuts are strong (up to W·m).
- For the design that uses every opposite pair equally often (sources spread evenly; the optimal cut measure can
  then be taken invariant under the group), the best cut bound is
  M ≥ W·max_S [P(S separates an opposite pair)/P(S separates an edge)]. Computed [`calc/frames.py` (e)]: the spectral ceiling over all cuts equals the point cuts {L ∋ v},
  ratio 2 − 2^{1−m}: **1.5 at m = 2 and 1.75 at m = 3.** So cut counting alone cannot exclude a common-frame saving
  with spread sources even at block 2, where Theorem R proves 2W.
- Reading [read: the network-coding citation note of an earlier round, rows on hub #288; an earlier note on hub #288]: a common-frame
  network is a one-shot linear network code on the undirected graph of frames, with W sessions between opposite
  frames; Δ is the coding gain over routing. This is hub #288's reading; nothing new is claimed for it here.

## 2a. The widened game (task 1, second half)

In the widened game every monomial matrix with entries in {1, i, −1, −i} is free (group B′). Put **frame of g** :=
the coset B′g, and let the frame graph have an edge between B′g and B′hg for every directional kernel h. Then free
steps fix frames, a paid move changes the frame by exactly one edge (h ∉ B′), right multiplication is an isometry,
and the distance from the raw frame B′ to the kernel frame B′T is m (at least log₂ of the row support of T,
which doubles at most per move — bm.py's `dist` — and at most m). Definition 2, Theorem 1 (with dim(a ∩ e) read as
m − d(a, e)), Propositions 2 and 3, Corollaries 1, 2 and 2′, and Theorem 5 hold verbatim, since their proofs use
only these four facts [proved]. Only the subspace-frame formulas and the cut computation are specific to the
Lagrangian graph.

## 3. Every unit: the sharing bound

**Theorem 5** [proved]. For every unit (any engine word; any contents, several terms per input allowed), each
input s has a *contributing history* π_s — a sequence of arrays, jumping only at gates, along which the product
of the letters applied equals the final element p·T — and π_s passes at least m paid moves. With μ_j the number
of inputs whose chosen history passes paid move j:

  Σ_j μ_j ≥ W·m,   hence Δ ≤ Σ_j (μ_j − 1).

*Proof.* Expand the (r_out, s) entry of the product of the step matrices: it is a sum over index sequences
(histories) of (scalar) × (product of the letters met). The sum equals c·p·T with c ≠ 0, and group elements are
a basis of C[G] (Fact 0), so some history has product exactly p·T. Its frame starts at λ(e) = L_0, ends at
λ(pT) = L_T, and moves by one step per paid move and by none otherwise (Lemma 1), so it meets ≥ m paid moves. Sum
over s. ∎
- Any saving therefore needs paid moves that serve several inputs' contributing histories at once. This holds in
  every game whose frame graph puts the target at distance m (the widened game too), and for mixed targets with m
  replaced by each target's rank. It is the most general form of "data must share moves"; it excludes nothing by
  itself (μ_j can be as large as W).

## 4. Where Theorem 1 stops

**4.1 Additions between different frames.** In C[G] an addition between an array at frame λ(g) and one at frame
λ(g') with g' ∉ C·g is an addition whose coefficient is the operator g'g⁻¹: the resulting row carries two elements,
so no twist makes it single-term [proved, from the definition]. (The same identity in Lean is `multiAct_mulR`,
used by `GRoute.reframe` [read: proveout NOTE line 94].) The engine accepts such additions at no cost. Units that
use them are outside Definition 2, and Theorem 1 says nothing about them.

**4.2 Re-framing.** `GRoute.reframe` ("Same word, same price") changes the twist of some inputs between stages.
In Definition 2's terms: a twist k_s may be changed at a time when input s sits alone in one array (a restored
helper); the content is unchanged and that array's frame path jumps by an isometry. A unit that does this is a
common-frame network piece by piece, not globally; its arrays' accounting is per session. Moves are still
counted correctly and Theorem 5 still holds; Theorem 1 holds per session [argued].

**4.3 Units with several terms per input** (the register model in general): rows with terms at several frames.
A paid move advances every term at once; a gate may move terms between arrays at any frames. No per-array
accounting exists; Theorem 5 and Theorem R's certificates are the only general statements.

**Proposition 6 (what a general accounting would decide)** [proved from Corollary 2 and the MAP's status table].
Call "the accounting general for W arrays" the statement: every engine unit with W arrays costs at least as much as
the cheapest common-frame network with W arrays at the same block. For W = 2 that statement is M ≥ 2m at every
block (Corollary 2). It would therefore settle, negatively, 2 in 7 at block 4 and every open two-array case at
blocks 5 and 6 (listed under Corollary 2; 41W/13, 2.99172 and 2.97290 per array and the two-layer bound do not
exclude them). Each of those is open after
the certificate programme, beam searches and lifts (MAP). So a general accounting is at least as strong as the
open conjecture "two arrays always need 2m moves", which is one of the moonshot questions itself. By Corollaries 2
and 2′ that conjecture is now proved for common-frame networks and for single-term contents; what is left of it is
exactly the units with several terms per input.
At block 3 with any W: if common-frame networks cannot save there (expected by every argument on record: Corollary
2 for W = 2; #288's small cases; an-synth's walls), generality would give M ≥ 3W, beyond the ceiling 169/57 per
array of all Theorem-R certificates (pf-duality D3, unchecked). No method we have proves anything of that size.
