[computed] (a program in this folder and its output), [cited] (a published theorem, cited from memory),
[read] (taken from one of our notes), [argued]. Programs: `ledger.py` (engine, ledger, coherence tests),
`t_known.py`, `t_strict.py`, `t_strictledger.py`, `t_pauliblind.py`, `t_random.py`, `t_exhaust.py`, `t_census.py`,

## 0. Setting

The engine game at block m, as in `../../moonshot/theory-guide/RULES.md` section 0 and `hb.py`.

- **Letters.** Paid: dir z = ca·1 + cb·X^z (z ≠ 0 in F_2^m, ca = (1+i)/2, cb = (1−i)/2). Free: shifts X^z, invertible
  address maps, phase letters diag(i^((z·x + c) mod 2)), scalars. G is the group they generate; B is the subgroup
  generated by the free letters. K = dir e_1 ⋯ dir e_m.
- **Contents.** Register r holds a formal sum c_r = Σ c_(g,l) g y_l over terms (g, l), g ∈ G, l an input. A word
  that is right at every number of digit columns is right as a formal identity (Vandermonde, column-uniform letters)
  [read: RULES section 0; ../../exist2 NOTE section 4].
- **Steps.** move (c_r := p c_r, p paid), free (c_r := b c_r), mix (c_r := Σ_s μ_s b_s c_s, old values, μ_s scalars,
  b_s free or 1), gate (several registers replaced at once by scalar combinations).
- **Unit.** The inputs y_1..y_W start in registers 1..W; other registers start empty (scratch). At the end every
  delivering register r holds exactly u_r K y_π(r), with u_r a unit scalar and π a bijection onto the inputs.
  Scratch may end as anything. M is the number of moves. The saving in moves is Δ := Wm − M.

## 1. Paid length, labels, levels, positions

**Lemma 1.1 (paid length)** [proved]. Let ℓ(g) be the least number of paid letters in a word equal to g up to a scalar
(free letters cost nothing). Then:
- (a) ℓ(b g b') = ℓ(g) for b, b' ∈ B, and ℓ(g⁻¹) = ℓ(g);
- (b) ℓ(p g) − ℓ(g) ∈ {−1, 0, +1} for a paid letter p;
- (c) ℓ(gh) ≤ ℓ(g) + ℓ(h);
- (d) in the engine game, ℓ(g) = d(ḡ L0, L0) = rank over F_2 of the x-block of ḡ on L0. Here ḡ ∈ Sp(2m, 2) is the
  action of g on Pauli labels (a|b), L0 = {(0|b)} is the raw frame, and d(E, E') = m − dim(E ∩ E') is the distance of
  the dual polar graph Γ_m of Lagrangian subspaces of F_2^(2m);
- (e) ℓ(K) = m.

*Proof.* (a) Left invariance is the definition. A word for g read backwards with every letter inverted is a word
for g⁻¹, and (dir z)⁻¹ = X^z·dir z is one paid letter times a free one, so ℓ(g⁻¹) ≤ ℓ(g) and by symmetry equality;
right invariance follows: ℓ(g b') = ℓ(b'⁻¹ g⁻¹) = ℓ(g⁻¹) = ℓ(g). (b) ℓ(pg) ≤ ℓ(g) + 1, and g = p⁻¹(pg) with p⁻¹
costing one paid letter. (c) Concatenate words. (d) Each paid letter acts on labels as the transvection T_(z|0):
(a|b) ↦ (a + (z·b) z | b), which moves every Lagrangian by distance at most 1; every free letter fixes L0. So
d(ḡ L0, L0) ≤ ℓ(g). Conversely, the images of the free letters generate the whole stabiliser P of L0: address maps
give the Levi factor GL(m, 2); phase letters give the transformations (a|b) ↦ (a | b + (z·a) z), whose products give
every (a|b) ↦ (a | b + S a) with S symmetric; and the kernel of g ↦ ḡ (Paulis and scalars) is free (Z^z is the square
of a phase letter up to sign). The parabolic P acts transitively on the Lagrangians at each distance k from L0, and
T_(e1|0)⋯T_(ek|0) sends L0 to one at distance k. So ḡ ∈ P̄·T_(e1)⋯T_(ek)·P̄ with k = d(ḡ L0, L0), and ℓ(g) ≤ k.
(e) K̄ has x-block I on L0, of rank m (also `ledger.py`, `E.Ksym`). ∎

In the widened game define ℓ by the same minimum; (a)–(c) hold by the same proof, and (e) is the statement that one
array needs m moves there [read: MAP 1.2, the filtered rung closes 1-array units]. Nothing below uses (d) except the
computations.

**Definition 1.2 (gauge, labels, positions).** A *gauge* is a choice of h_1, …, h_W ∈ G, one per input. Fix a normal
subgroup 𝒩 of G contained in B·C^×. Two choices are used:
- 𝒩 = C^×, giving *strict labels*;
- in the engine game, 𝒩 = Paulis·C^×, giving *frame labels* (a label is then an element of Sp(2m, 2)).

The *label* of a term (g, l) is λ = g h_l modulo 𝒩.
- Its *level* is δ(λ) = ℓ(λ), well defined by Lemma 1.1(a).
- Its *position* is the coset Bλ. In the engine game this is the Lagrangian λ̄⁻¹ L0 ∈ Γ_m.
- Distances: d(Bλ, Bμ) := ℓ(λ μ⁻¹), well defined and a metric by Lemma 1.1(a, c).
- Λ_r(t) is the set of labels of the terms of register r after step t.

For an input a:
- the *source label* is h_a (the label of the raw term (1, a)), with source position S_a and source level σ_a = ℓ(h_a);
- the *sink label* is K h_a, with sink position T_a and sink level τ_a = ℓ(K h_a);
- d(S_a, T_a) = ℓ(K) = m, so source and sink are always *opposite*.

**Lemma 1.3 (lockstep)** [proved]. A paid letter p on register r sends every label λ ∈ Λ_r to pλ, and Λ_r(new) =
pΛ_r with no merging. Each label changes level by −1, 0 or +1 and its position moves to an adjacent position
(distance exactly 1). A free letter b sends Λ_r to bΛ_r and fixes every level and every position.

*Proof.* g ↦ pg is injective on terms and commutes with the quotient by the normal subgroup 𝒩. Levels: Lemma 1.1(b).
Positions: d(Bpλ, Bλ) = ℓ(p) = 1. Free letters: Bbλ = Bλ, and ℓ(bλ) = ℓ(λ). ∎

**Definition 1.4 (the ledger quantities).**
- For each pair (paid move on r, label λ ∈ Λ_r) the pair is an *ascent*, a *lateral* or a *descent* as δ goes up,
  stays or goes down. A, Z and D count them.
- *Packing:* P := Σ over moves with Λ_r ≠ ∅ of (|Λ_r| − 1), the extra labels a move advances for free.
- *Idle moves:* I0 := the number of moves on empty registers.
- Ψ(t) := Σ_r Σ_{λ ∈ Λ_r(t)} δ(λ), the total level held.
- *Branching at mixes:* B_mix := − (sum over mix and gate steps of the change of Ψ). It measures level destroyed
  (junk cancelled, overwrites) minus level created (copies).
- For a unit:
  - N := Σ_a σ_a, the anatomy's "sum of source-label dimensions";
  - X := Σ_a (σ_a + τ_a − m) ≥ 0, the off-geodesic defect (triangle inequality through the reference frame);
  - B := B_mix + Ψ_end − Σ_{r delivering} τ_π(r), the branching including what scratch and junk carry away at
    the end.

## 2. The ledger identity

**Theorem 2.1 (every word, every gauge, either kind of label)** [proved].
  M = Ψ_end − Ψ_0 + B_mix + 2D + Z − P + I0.

*Proof (induction on the word).* Let ΔΨ_σ be the change of Ψ at step σ.
- A move on a register with k ≥ 1 labels: by Lemma 1.3, ΔΨ_σ = a_σ − d_σ, with a_σ + d_σ + z_σ = k. So
  1 = ΔΨ_σ + 2d_σ + z_σ − (k − 1).
- A move on an empty register: ΔΨ_σ = 0 and it counts 1 in I0.
- A free letter: ΔΨ_σ = 0 (Lemma 1.3).
- Mixes and gates: ΔΨ_σ is whatever it is, and it is booked in B_mix.

Summing 1 over the moves gives M = Σ_moves ΔΨ + 2D + Z − P + I0, and Σ_moves ΔΨ = Ψ_end − Ψ_0 + B_mix. ∎

**Corollary 2.2 (every unit)** [proved].
  M = W·m − 2N + X + 2D + Z + B − P + I0,  that is,  Δ = 2N − X − 2D − Z − B + P − I0.

*Proof.* Scratch starts empty, so Ψ_0 = Σ_a σ_a = N. By definition Ψ_end = Σ_{r delivering} τ_π(r) + (B − B_mix),
and Σ_{r delivering} τ_π(r) = Σ_a τ_a = Wm − N + X. Substitute in Theorem 2.1. ∎

**Corollary 2.3 (the raw gauge: every saving is net packing)** [proved]. In the trivial gauge (h_a = 1) N = X = 0,
so for every unit

  Δ = P − B − 2D − Z − I0  ≤  P − B.

A unit saves only if some paid moves act on registers holding terms at several labels, and the extra labels they
advance are not all destroyed later at a higher level than they were packed at.

**Corollary 2.4 (the anatomy identity and its exact hypotheses)** [proved]. Suppose some gauge makes:
- (H1) every register hold at most one label at every time (*coherent*);
- (H2) B = 0: no label is born or destroyed at a mix, and no scratch label is left;
- (H3) Z = 0 and X = 0.

Then M = Wm − 2N + 2D, the anatomy's M = Wm − 2N + 2L with L = D. Under (H1) to (H3), M ≡ Wm (mod 2).

**Lemma 2.5 (what X = 0 means)** [proved]. In the engine game, X_a = 0 if and only if the frame F = h̄_a L0 lies on a
geodesic from L0 to L1 = K̄ L0, if and only if F = F_C := {(c | a + c) : c ∈ C, a ∈ C^⊥} for a subspace C ⊆ F_2^m
(any subspace, degenerate allowed). Then σ_a = dim C, and the sink frame is F_(C^⊥).
- OpenAI's labels Φ_U with orthonormal U are the case C = U, F_U = graph(P_U).

*Proof.* σ_a = d(F, L0) and τ_a = d(F, L1) (Lemma 1.1(d) and K̄² = 1 mod 2), and d(L0, L1) = m. Geodesic points
between opposite Lagrangians L0 and L1 are exactly the F with F = (F ∩ L0) ⊕ (F ∩ L1). Write F ∩ L1 =
{(c|c) : c ∈ C}. Isotropy against (0|a) forces a ∈ C^⊥, and dimensions add to m. K̄ maps (c | a + c) to (a | a + c),
which is F_(C^⊥). ∎

**Remark 2.6.** (H1) is the anatomy's "every gate joins arrays at one common frame". (H2) is "labels only change
by moves" with no scratch. (H3) is "sink = complement of source, each move one dimension".
- These are hypotheses about how the three known savers were *built* (they were designed in OpenAI's frame
  calculus and the hub's frame templates).
- They are not facts about units in general. A unit that violates them is not a contradiction of the identity. It
  is simply booked by Corollary 2.2 with B, P, X or Z non-zero.

## 3. Gauges, coherence, and the precise meaning of "second mechanism"

**Proposition 3.1 (gauge exchange)** [proved; computed instance].
- M is gauge independent; N, X, D, Z, B and P are not. Changing the gauge moves saving between the crossing term 2N
  and the packing term P.
- In particular, every known saver (all coherent in their designers' gauge, with P = 0) shows its saving as packing
  P > 0 in the raw gauge.
- Computed instance (`t_known.out`, "gauge-exchange", blocks 2 to 4): a line source for input 2 paid back by one
  descent. In the coherent gauge (N, D, P, B) = (1, 1, 0, 0); in the raw gauge (0, 0, m−1, m−1). M = 2m in both.

*Proof.* M is a count of letters. The other quantities depend on h through the labels. For the coherent savers, in
the raw gauge a register holding data of inputs with different source labels holds several labels (Φ_L Φ_(U_a) for
different a), so its moves count in P. ∎

**Definition 3.2.** A word is *coherent in gauge h* if |Λ_r(t)| ≤ 1 for every register and time. It is
*(strictly / frame-) coherent* if some gauge makes it so, with strict or frame labels. Liveness: a register value is
*live* if it reaches a delivering register's final value in the word's data-flow graph. Deleting the steps that
produce no live value leaves a unit with no more moves. So "the live part is coherent" is the hypothesis that matters
below.

**Proposition 3.3 (the two obstructions)** [proved; algorithm in `ledger.find_coherent_gauge`,
`ledger.find_strict_gauge`]. A word is coherent in some gauge exactly when both of the following hold:
- (O1) no register ever holds two terms (g, l), (g', l) of the same input with g, g' in different 𝒩-classes. This is
  the *same-input split*, and it is gauge invariant.
- (O2) the relative gauges forced by co-occurring inputs are consistent: whenever a register holds (g, l) and
  (g', l'), require h_l' = g'⁻¹ g h_l; these requirements must agree around every cycle. This is the *holonomy*
  condition.

*Proof.* Coherence means g h_l = g' h_l' for all terms of a register at a time. For l = l' that is (O1). For l ≠ l'
it is a voltage-graph equation on the inputs, solvable exactly when the voltage around every closed walk is trivial;
breadth-first propagation from one root per component decides it. ∎

**The precise statement behind "is crossing slack the only mechanism?"** By Corollary 2.3 every saving is net
packing in the raw gauge. *Crossing slack* is packing that a gauge removes (coherent words). A *second mechanism*
would be a saving unit whose live part is incoherent in every gauge. With strict labels there are two sub-classes:
- (S1) *frame-incoherent*: a same-input split or holonomy already in Sp(2m, 2) (the order-3 control below);
- (S2) *Pauli-blind packing*: frame-coherent but not strictly coherent. Some live array holds two terms of one input
  whose group elements differ by a Pauli, as in (1 + Z^v) g y. These ride on one frame, so the frame ledger sees
  no packing, but the exact labels differ. This is the "Pauli-blind difference" channel of RULES H1.4.

Theorem 4.3 below excludes two-array savers outside (S1); more precisely it requires frame packing on a live move
in every gauge. Sub-class (S2) is closed by the module version of Lemma 4.2.

**Example 3.4 (a known exact unit that breaks the anatomy identity's form)** [computed, `t_known.out`,
`t_strict.out`, `t_random.out`].
- The order-3 control of o3-build (C8O3 at block 4, and its block-3 analogue C6O3) does this:
  1. r1 := x1 + x2, r2 := x1 − s x2, with s a 3-cycle of coordinates (s K = K s);
  2. K on both registers;
  3. the readout K x2 = (1 − s + s²)/2 (r1 − r2), K x1 = r1 − r2.
- It is an exact unit with holonomy: no gauge, strict or frame, is coherent.
- Raw gauge: P = B = m (the label K s̄ of r2 is advanced for free by m moves and destroyed at full level m by the
  readout). Δ = 0.
- 270 randomised variants (W = 2, 3, random 3-cycles, move orders and interleavings) all give P = B = (W − 1)m.
- This is "pack, move once, unpack" with an exact refund.

## 4. Coherent units are undirected network codes; what that proves

Let a unit have *no frame packing* in an Sp-gauge h: whenever a paid move acts on a live register, all terms of
that register share one frame label (P_frame = 0 on live moves). Between moves a register may hold several frame
classes, and then each class is treated as a separate *virtual sub-register* at its own position. Frame-coherent
words are the special case where every register has one class at all times.
- Let 𝒫 be the Pauli group (with its phases), normal in G and contained in B, and let 𝒜 be the algebra spanned by
  𝒫 (the algebra of all 2^m × 2^m matrices).
- Choose for each live sub-register an exact representative λ of its frame class, and update it with the register's
  letters (λ ↦ pλ, λ ↦ bλ).
- Every term (g, l) of the sub-register then has g = λ P h_l⁻¹ with P ∈ 𝒫. So its part of c_r is
  λ Σ_l A_l h_l⁻¹ y_l, with a *virtual content* v = (A_1, …, A_W) ∈ 𝒜^W.
- Under strict coherence every A_l is a scalar and v ∈ C^W.

**Lemma 4.1 (the virtual network)** [proved].
- (i) Moves and free letters do not change v_r. The letter acts on λ_r, to the left of the Pauli part.
- (ii) A live mix writes into each frame class of r an element of the left 𝒜-submodule generated by the virtual
  contents of the summands' sub-registers of that class, all of which sit at that class's position. Classes do not
  interact.
- (iii) Input a starts with v = e_a at S_a. Delivering register r ends at T_π(r) with v = (unit Pauli)·e_π(r), which
  generates 𝒜 e_π(r).

*Proof.* (i) dir z·λ_r P h⁻¹ = (dir z·λ_r) P h⁻¹, and the same for free letters.

(ii) Group the summands μ_s b_s c_s by the frame class of b_s λ_s.
- Distinct classes give distinct group elements for each input, so no cancellation happens between classes.
- Within one class λ, a summand sub-register with b_s λ_s = λ Q_s (Q_s ∈ 𝒫, λ the result's representative)
  contributes μ_s Q_s v_s.
- Members of a class share the position of λ (𝒫 ⊆ B). A move acts on a register with one class only (the
  hypothesis), so (i) applies to it.

(iii) Definitions. ∎

**Lemma 4.2 (cut condition)** [proved]. For every set Q of positions, the number of moves that cross between Q and its
complement (in either direction) is at least the number of inputs a with exactly one of S_a, T_a in Q.

*Proof.* Let V_Q(t) be the left 𝒜-submodule of 𝒜^W generated by the virtual contents of live sub-registers positioned
in Q, and let len be composition length (𝒜 = M_n(C) with n = 2^m, so len(𝒜) = n and a cyclic module 𝒜v has
len ≤ n). By Lemma 4.1:
- free letters and mixes never enlarge V_Q. A mix result inside Q lies in the submodule of the summands inside Q,
  and a register overwritten from elsewhere only leaves.
- A move enlarges V_Q only when it enters Q, and then by the cyclic module of the moving vector: at most n in length.

So V_Q(end) ⊆ V_Q(0) + Σ_entering 𝒜 v_w. Here V_Q(0) = ⊕_{S_a ∈ Q} 𝒜 e_a, and V_Q(end) ⊇ ⊕_{T_a ∈ Q} 𝒜 e_a. Comparing
lengths:

  n · #(entering moves) ≥ n · #{a : T_a ∈ Q, S_a ∉ Q}.

The same argument for the complement gives leaving moves ≥ #{a : S_a ∈ Q, T_a ∉ Q}. ∎

(Under strict coherence the same proof runs with ordinary spans in C^W.)

**Theorem 4.3 (a unit with one or two arrays saves only by packing frames)** [proved, using Hu's theorem].
- Let a unit with W ≤ 2, at any block and with any amount of scratch, have an Sp-gauge with no frame packing: every
  paid move on a live array acts on terms that share one frame label. Then M ≥ Wm.
- Equivalently: a two-array saver has frame packing (P_frame > 0 on live moves) in every Sp-gauge.
- The same proof works in the widened game with labels taken modulo the largest normal subgroup of G inside B, given
  Lemma 1.1(e) there.

*Proof.* W = 1 is the case below with one demand (max-flow min-cut). For W = 2:
1. Let H be the graph whose vertices are the positions visited and whose edges join positions at distance 1. Give
   edge e the capacity c(e) = the number of moves along e, in either direction.
2. Lemma 4.2 is exactly the cut condition for the two demands (S_1, T_1), (S_2, T_2) of value 1.
3. By Hu's two-commodity flow theorem (T. C. Hu, Operations Research 11, 1963) [cited], an undirected network
   satisfying the cut condition for two commodities carries a feasible fractional two-commodity flow f_1 + f_2 ≤ c.
4. Every unit of f_a travels along a path of H from S_a to T_a, whose length is at least d(S_a, T_a) = m.
5. Hence M ≥ Σ_e c(e) ≥ Σ_e (f_1(e) + f_2(e)) ≥ 2m. ∎

The theorem generalises:
- the anatomy's "two arrays need 2m" (an-openai 2.3: one meeting point, no scratch, its labels);
- MAP 1.3's "single-term contents: 2 arrays need 2m".

Multi-term contents are allowed here: Pauli-blind twins, junk, any number of inputs per array, and several frames
in one array between moves. All that is required is that no paid move acts on an array whose terms sit on two
frames, in some gauge.

**Remark 4.3b (grouped moves).** A rank-r move dir z_1 ⋯ dir z_r on one array equals r consecutive rank-1 moves on
that array, since directions commute; it carries the same frame classes throughout. So Theorem 4.3 bounds the
*total rank*: Σ ranks ≥ Wm for W ≤ 2 without frame packing.
- In particular a coherent grouped 2 in 7 at block 4 (ranks 2, 2, 2, 1, total 7 < 8) is excluded.

**Remark 4.3a (what the hypothesis leaves out).**
- Paulis·scalars is the largest normal subgroup of G inside B in the engine game (the parabolic of Sp(2m, 2) has
  trivial core). So frame labels are the coarsest labels this argument can use.
- Two-array savers must therefore pack frames: in every Sp-gauge some paid move acts on a live array whose terms
  sit on two or more frames. Pauli twins on one frame (S2) and multi-frame contents that are never moved do not
  suffice.
- Input relabellings with group-algebra coefficients (o3-build's Lemma 1: x = F⁻¹ y, F ∈ GL_W(Q[R]), R the free
  letters commuting with K) can turn an (S1) word into a frame-coherent unit for the targets K y. The order-3 control
  is such a word: in the basis y it is the plain schedule.
- Theorem 4.3 then applies to the relabelled word. So a two-array saver must pack frames after every such
  relabelling as well.

**Corollary 4.4 (other demand layouts)** [proved for (a); cited for (b)].
- (a) If all source positions coincide, or all sink positions coincide, M ≥ Wm. (Single-commodity max-flow min-cut
  with a super-sink or super-source.)
- (b) More generally M ≥ Wm whenever the demand graph (positions, with an edge S_a–T_a per input) has a form for
  which the cut condition is known to suffice. By the Papernov–Lomonosov classification this means a subgraph of
  K4, of C5, or of a union of two stars [cited from memory; not re-read].

**Theorem 4.5 (general W: savers without frame packing lose at most a log factor)** [proved, using the multicommodity
flow-cut gap, cited].
- Every unit with an Sp-gauge without frame packing on live moves has M ≥ Wm / (C·log(2W)) for an absolute constant C. (Linial–London–
  Rabinovich 1995; Aumann–Rabani 1998: under the cut condition an undirected network routes a 1/O(log k) fraction of
  all k demands concurrently; then argue as in 4.3.)
- So a coherent unit with saving s at block m needs log(2W) ≥ m^s / C: astronomically many arrays as m^s grows.
- At the blocks we care about this is no constraint (m^0.1 = 1.54 at m = 73), so it does not bear on 0.1.

**Remark 4.6 (cut arguments are weak for three or more arrays)** [computed].
- Averaging over Sp(2m, 2), the best configuration-free cut bound is M ≥ c_m·W, where
  c_m = max over vertex sets Q of Γ_m of (share of opposite pairs split by Q) / (share of edges cut by Q).
- `cutconst.py` finds:
  - c_2 = 1.5 exactly (all 16,383 sets);
  - c_3 ≥ 1.75, with balls, Voronoi half-spaces, vector sets and 40 local-search restarts finding nothing better.
  - Both equal 2(1 − 2^−m), the "colouring" level of MAP 1.2.
- `cutlp.py` at block 2:
  - the cut LP equals 4 = 2m on every two-pair configuration (Hu, checked);
  - it drops to 1.83, 1.875 and 1.75 per pair for some 6-, 8- and 12-pair configurations;
  - it is 1.5 per pair for all 120 ordered opposite pairs.
- So for W ≥ 3, cuts cannot exclude coherent savers even at block 2, where Theorem 2W does. Coherence alone does not
  give the block-3 or block-4 floors. Theorem R stays the stronger tool there.

**Remark 4.7 (the network-coding reading)** [proved].
- A coherent unit is a scalar linear network code for W unicast sessions (S_a → T_a, opposite pairs) on the
  undirected graph Γ_m, costed by edge uses.
- Integral or fractional routing costs exactly Wm.
- Conversely, every scalar linear network code over C on Γ_m for opposite-pair sessions is a coherent engine unit
  with M = edge uses. A step to an adjacent position is a paid letter times free letters, since ℓ(g) = 1 exactly on
